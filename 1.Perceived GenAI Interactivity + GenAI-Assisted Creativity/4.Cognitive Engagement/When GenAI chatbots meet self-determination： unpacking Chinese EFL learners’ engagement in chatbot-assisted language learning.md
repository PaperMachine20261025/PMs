ABSTRACT While generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) chatbots show transformative potentials in English as a foreign language (EFL) education, critical gaps persist in understanding how chatbot-assisted language learning affect multidimensional learner engagement. Grounded in self-determination theory (SDT), this sequential explanatory mixedmethods study examines the predictive effect of perceived supports on learners’ engagement in chatbot-assisted language learning, and the mediating role of basic psychological needs (BPNs) in the relationship. Data from 506 Chinese EFL learners (quantitative phase: structural equation modeling of survey responses) and 18 followup interviewees (qualitative phase: thematic analysis) indicated that: (1) perceived supports had a significant and positive impact on cognitive and emotional engagement, as opposed to behavioral and agentic engagement; (2) perceived supports significantly and positively predicted BPNs (autonomy, competence, and relatedness); (3) BPNs significantly and positively influenced behavioral, cognitive, emotional, and agentic engagement; and (4) perceived supports indirectly affected emotional and agentic engagement through autonomy, and behavioral engagement through relatedness, while competence consistently mediated all four engagement dimensions. Furthermore, qualitative insights from the semi-structured interviews triangulated the quantitative results, offering a comprehensive understanding of EFL learners’ engagement. The findings shed light on the future research avenues and the

practical deployment of GenAI chatbots in EFL education.

KEYWORDS Generative AI chatbots; basic psychological needs (BPNs); Chatbot-assisted language learning; engagement; selfdetermination theory

Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) chatbots are AI-powered conversational systems that utilize deep learning neural networks and pre-trained large language models built on extensive online corpora to generate human-like responses (Kohnke et al., 2023). With the capacity to simulate immersive conversational experiences, generate contextually relevant responses, and offer personalized language practice, GenAI chatbots have demonstrated considerable potential in facilitating English as a foreign language (EFL) learning (Li et al., 2024; Liu & Ma, 2024; Wang et al., 2024). Grounded in self-determination theory (SDT, Deci & Ryan, 1985), recent studies show that GenAI chatbots not only offer perceived supports to EFL learners (Shah et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2023), such as providing options, giving immediate feedback, and simulating human conversation, but they also help fulfill essential psychological needs by encouraging autonomy (Alamri et al., 2020), enhancing competence (Chiu et al., 2024), and nurturing relatedness (Dizon, 2024).

Despite growing evidence that GenAI chatbots contribute positively to learners’ BPNs, recent research has largely concentrated on exploring engagement in chatbot-assisted language learning (Wang & Wang, 2024; Wang & Xue, 2024; Yang & Li, 2024a). Such emphasis is well-justified, as engagement is widely recognized as a key predictor of learning persistence, the deployment of effective learning strategies, and enhanced self-efficacy, which collectively contribute to improved academic performance (Carmona-Halty et al., 2021; Yang & Li, 2024b). For example, drawing on the SDT, Wang and Wang (2024) employed a mixed-methods approach to investigate EFL learners’ engagement with GenAI chatbots, finding that BPNs play a critical

Although prior studies have examined learners’ perceived supports, BPNs, and engagement in chatbotassisted language learning, critical gaps remain in fully understanding the interrelationships among these variables. First, while existing studies have looked into the impact of chatbots on learner engagement (Guo et al., 2022; Luo & Zou, 2024), there is a lack of research specifically targeting GenAI chatbots in EFL environments, highlighting the necessity for further investigation. Second, many studies conducted so far have approached BPNs or engagement as integrated constructs (Chen & Jang, 2010; Xia et al., 2023), disregarding the specific functions of individual psychological needs in determining the four dimensions of engagement. Third and importantly, while perceived supports, BPNs, and engagement have all been studied individually, there is a lack of research that combines these elements into a cohesive framework, leaving the mediating influence of BPNs on the relationship between perceived support and engagement largely unexamined. To address these gaps, guided by the SDT, this study aims to investigate the relationships among perceived supports, BPNs (autonomy, competence, and relatedness), and Chinese EFL learners’ engagement (behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and agentic) in chatbot-assisted language learning. Accordingly, three research questions are proposed:

(1) What are the effects of perceived supports on BPNs and engagement in chatbot-assisted language

learning? (2) What are the effects of BPNs on engagement in chatbot-assisted language learning? (3) What are the mediating effects of BPNs on the relationship between perceived supports and engage-

ment in chatbot-assisted language learning?

Literature review and research hypotheses

Self-determination theory

Self-Determination Theory (SDT), proposed by Deci and Ryan (1985), provides a robust framework for understanding learner engagement. According to the SDT, individuals possess basic psychological needs (BPNs), and when learners perceive contextual supports that satisfy these needs, they are more likely to exhibit sustained engagement (Reeve, 2013; Skinner & Belmont, 1993). With the growing integration of GenAI technologies, SDT has been extensively applied not only in traditional face-to-face educational contexts (Ryan & Deci, 2017, 2020), but also in emerging studies that explore how GenAI chatbots can facilitate learner engagement in chatbot-assisted language learning (Li et al., 2024; Wang & Wang, 2024; Xia et al., 2024).

Recent investigations have aimed to align SDT with the GenAI environment, positing that learners’ BPNs are shaped by the specific teaching advantages of GenAI chatbots, such as tailored topics, feedback on skills, and language that expresses emotions (Li et al., 2024). Drawing on the SDT, perceived supports and BPNs are considered critical constructs for understanding learner engagement in GenAI chatbots learning (Reeve, 2013). Perceived supports refer to learners’ perceptions of the assistance provided by GenAI chatbots throughout the learning process, which has been identified as a key factor in supporting academic success (Lee et al., 2011). BPNs represent three universal, innate, and essential needs – autonomy, competence, and relatedness – that serve as fundamental drivers of engagement (Reeve, 2012). Informed by Reeve’s (2013) engagement framework and prior empirical findings (Chiu et al., 2024; Li & Chiu, 2025), the following relationships are hypothesized: (1) perceived supports have a positive and direct effect on BPNs; (2) BPNs have a positive and direct effect on engagement; and (3) perceived supports have a positive and direct effect on engagement.

Perceived supports in chatbot-assisted language learning

Perceived supports encompass the diverse supportive behaviors that learners perceive as facilitating their learning in a course context (Lee et al., 2011). Grounded in the SDT, these supports are manifested in GenAI chatbots encouraging EFL learners to pursue personal goals and exercise choice in their learning, thereby promoting autonomy (Alamri et al., 2020), providing clear instructions and personalized feedback

Over the past years, researchers have begun examining the effects of perceived supports on BPNs in GenAI chatbot-assisted language learning (Luo & Zou, 2024; Shah et al., 2024). For example, Shah et al. (2024) highlighted that the perceived supports from ChatGPT positively influence learners’ BPNs in GenAI chatbots learning activities. It was found that GenAI chatbots enable EFL learners to inquire at their own pace, which fosters autonomy, delivering personalized feedback to improve their competence, and facilitating collaboration with peers to enhance their sense of relatedness. Similarly, Luo and Zou (2024) explored EFL learners’ BPNs in GenAI chatbots learning activities, revealing that the ChatGPT’s generation of tailored materials led to notable improvements in their reading and speaking skills, along with fostering greater language learning autonomy. In addition, individualized support throughout the learning process was shown to strengthen learners’ sense of relatedness. H2. Perceived supports significantly and positively influence competence.

H3. Perceived supports significantly and positively influence relatedness.

BPNs in chatbot-assisted language learning

According to the SDT, BPNs comprise autonomy, competence, and relatedness (Reeve, 2009; Ryan & Deci, 2017, 2020). In this study, autonomy is defined operationally by the features of GenAI chatbots that empower learners to choose discussion topics and manage the interaction pace independently. Competence is enhanced through prompt, tailored feedback that clarifies goals, reinforces appropriate use of expressions, and boosts learners’ confidence in communication. Additionally, relatedness is fostered by using socially attuned language and emotional signals (like emojis), which mimic human interaction and help create a feeling of interpersonal presence (Chiu, 2021, 2022). Existing research suggests that BPNs serve as direct predictors of learner engagement (Fang et al., 2019; Khan et al., 2018). First, when learners experience autonomy, they are granted greater flexibility in selecting learning goals, which enhances their capacity for time management and sustained attention, thereby reinforcing behavioral engagement (Alamri et al., 2020). Engagement in chatbot-assisted language learning

Engagement, conceptualized as “the extent of students’ active involvement in a learning activity” (Reeve, 2012, p. 150), has been categorized into behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and agentic dimensions (Fredricks et al., 2004; Reeve & Tseng, 2011). Specifically, behavioral engagement refers to the level of time and effort that language learners contribute to their GenAI chatbots learning activities, which is often demonstrated by their initiative in pursuing learning opportunities and their readiness to take responsibility for their own progress. Cognitive engagement entails the use of mental strategies, including reasoning, reflection, and analysis, as part of the GenAI chatbots learning experience. Learners who are cognitively involved usually show a keen interest in the content being taught and recognize the significance and purpose of their academic endeavors. Learners’ emotional engagement illustrates their feelings in GenAI chatbots learning settings, showing that those with significant emotional engagement usually describe sensations of safety, comfort, and a sincere enthusiasm for the learning atmosphere (Chiu, 2021; Fredricks et al., 2004). Unlike conventional chatbots that rely on predetermined scripts and have limited abilities to interpret learner input or respond to specific needs (Kohnke et al., 2023), GenAI chatbots are designed to generate context-sensitive and grammatically correct responses in real-time, allowing for a more fluid and adaptable learner interaction (Lo et al., 2024). This shift provides unique educational advantages, such as personalized feedback, enhanced coherence in interactions, and a more fulfilling emotional experience (Duong et al., 2023), thereby distinguishing GenAI chatbots from earlier rule-based systems in both instructional design and learner engagement (Yang & Li, 2024a). Recent empirical studies suggest that learners’ perceived supports in GenAI chatbot-assisted language learning may contribute to enhanced engagement (e.g. Khurma et al., 2024; Qi, 2024; Wang & Xue, 2024). In terms of behavioral engagement, Escalante et al. (2023) found that the continuous availability of GenAI chatbots offers on-demand support, thereby facilitating sustained interaction and promoting consistent participation in learning activities. With respect to cognitive engagement, Zhang and Tur (2024) reported that learners perceive GenAI chatbots as cognitive scaffolds that deliver tailored explanations and stimulate critical thinking. By evaluating chatbot-generated responses, learners engage in higher-order cognitive processes, which in turn reinforce their cognitive engagement. In addition, research indicates that GenAI chatbots can foster emotional engagement by establishing a supportive learning environment that mitigates anxiety and enhances motivation (Duong et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024). For example, Duong et al. (2023) found that students perceived GenAI chatbots as tools that facilitate social interaction, increase enjoyment, and reduce stress through collaborative learning. Although empirical studies on agentic engagement remain limited, the real-time interactivity and adaptive feedback provided by GenAI chatbots may empower learners to assume a more active role in directing their learning experiences (Chiu, 2022). Accordingly, the following hypotheses (H16 to H19) were proposed.

H16. Perceived supports significantly and positively influence behavioral engagement.

H17. Perceived supports significantly and positively influence cognitive engagement.

H18. Perceived supports significantly and positively influence emotional engagement.

H19. Perceived supports significantly and positively influence agentic engagement.

From the above discussion, it can be inferred that learners’ perceived supports have the capacity to fulfill their BPNs, which are critical for stimulating engagement (Khurma et al., 2024; Wang & Wang, 2024). When learners perceive supportive elements in their GenAI chatbots learning environment, their BPNs are more likely to be satisfied, leading to increased engagement in learning activities (Ryan & Deci, 2017). Therefore, this study, based on the principles of SDT, asserts that learners’ BPNs mediate the relationship between perceived supports and engagement.

Method

Research procedures

In this research project, a custom-built GenAI chatbot platform (see Appendix 1 for a complete depiction of the interface), referred to as XUEQING BOT, was designed to assist Chinese EFL learners in their communication skills. The system’s architecture that includes learner interface, processing module and data logging, shown in Figure 2, is designed to tackle the issue of technical transparency. The validity of this platform’s representativeness can be explained as follows: First, in contrast to other AI-assisted tools that cater to a broad audience without targeting specific language learner groups, XUEQING BOT offers meticulously crafted topics that allow Chinese EFL learners to choose topics they are already acquainted with and engage in conversations with the platform freely. Second, the abundant tailored feedback offered by the XUEQING BOT can provide substantial advantages for Chinese EFL learners. By leveraging OpenAI’s application programming interface (API), the system connects ChatGPT (GPT-4.0) to a web-based platform, allowing for the evaluation of learner input and the development of customized, context-relevant replies that focus on grammatical precision, word

choice, and expression suitability. Third, beyond its benefits for language development, this platform is intentionally designed to fulfill the basic needs of Chinese EFL learners, showcasing vital aspects such as a selection of recognized dialogue topics, individualized feedback that corresponds to learner responses, and the use of emojis to promote interactive and supportive conversations.

The study implemented a structured, progressive research procedures for data collection: First, before the experiment began, participants were informed about the study’s procedures and purpose. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, who were assured of confidentiality and anonymity. Second, all the participants autonomously interacted with the GenAI chatbot by logging in, choosing a discussion topic, and engaging in conversation. Each dialogue commenced with an opening question from the chatbot based on the selected topic and was sustained through follow-up inquiries generated in response to the learners’ input, utilizing the underlying large language model for contextual coherence. To support free-flowing interaction, the topics selected are ones that EFL learners are already acquainted with, including school life and academic dialogues, leisure activities and routine life, intercultural dialogue and viewpoint evaluation, and future planning and career development. Session durations were not predetermined, but learners were obligated to complete five turns of interaction before they could proceed. Third, after all the participants wrapped up their discussion with the chatbot, they were taken to the online questionnaire platform (https://www.wjx.cn/), with access permitted only after the interaction was complete, ensuring that all responses accurately depicted the real use of XUEQING BOT. Follow-up interviews (Appendix 3) were conducted with a group of chosen volunteers to uncover richer qualitative perspectives on their experiences.

Participants

A total of 577 Chinese EFL learners initially volunteered to participate in this survey. After eliminating 71 responses due to incorrect answers on trap questions, the final analysis included 506 valid surveys.

As apparent in Table 1, the participants included 182 males (36.0%) and 324 females (64.0%). Most respondents were aged 18–20 years (81.2%), followed by those aged 21–23 years (13.7%) and 16–17 years (5.2%). Among the four grades, 63.4% were first-year students, 22.9% were second-year students, 10.1% were thirdyear students, and 3.6% were in their fourth year.

Instruments

Data collection instruments consisted of items from a questionnaire survey (Appendix 2) and a semi-structured interview outline (Appendix 3). Alongside demographic characteristics like age, gender, and academic scores, the body part of the questionnaire featured three sections: perceived supports, BPNs, and engagement. All items adopted to measure the variables utilized a seven-point Likert-type scale (i.e. “strongly disagree” = 1 to “strongly agree” = 7). To validate the content of the scales, the first author translated the items into Chinese, and an English lecturer performed a back-translation. A professor in applied linguistics then assessed the scales, making minor changes to enhance clarity and uphold content validity.

Perceived supports The items of perceived supports were adapted from Lee et al. (2020). The four items measure learners’ perceived supports from the GenAI chatbot (e.g. “The GenAI chatbot allows me to choose the topic of conversation.”).

BPNs Nine items of BPNs were adapted from Hew and Kadir (2016), including autonomy (e.g. “I feel relaxed and autonomous when engaging in English communication with the GenAI chatbot.”), competence (e.g. “I experience a sense of achievement when communicating with the GenAI chatbot in English.”), and relatedness (e.g. “I feel supported during English conversations with the GenAI chatbot.”).

Engagement Twelve items of engagement in GenAI chatbot were adapted from Reeve and Tseng (2011), including three items of behavioral engagement (e.g. “I try to overcome difficulties during English conversations with the GenAI chatbot.”), three items of emotional engagement (e.g. “I feel happy and satisfied during English conversations with the GenAI chatbot.”), three items of cognitive engagement (e.g. “I integrate the English knowledge from the GenAI chatbot with my prior understanding.”), and three items of agentic engagement (e.g. “I express my preferences and opinions during English conversations with the GenAI chatbot.”).

Data analysis

Quantitative data analysis A two-step approach, as recommended by Anderson and Gerbing (1988), was adopted to evaluate the instruments and structural models sequentially. First, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was performed to ascertain the construct validity of the measurement model, assessing indicators such as composite reliability

Qualitative data analysis Qualitative data were analyzed using content analysis with MAXQDA 24.0, a qualitative data analysis software designed to support data organization, coding, and theme identification within narratives (Bazeley, 2013). Since this study is grounded in the SDT, a deductive content analysis was applied (Elo et al., 2014). The data were processed in three main phases. First, the researchers engaged with the qualitative data to achieve an early grasp of the subject matter. Second, a coding scheme was developed to highlight significant themes and guarantee that the data were categorized according to pre-established classifications. Two researchers independently analyzed the entire dataset using the scheme, and after the first round of coding, inter-rater reliability was assessed, resulting in a 94% agreement rate, which demonstrates a high level of consistency. Finally, the hypotheses were tested based on the analyzed data. Discrepancies were addressed through discussion and consensus, and the reconciliation process further clarified code definitions and refined thematic boundaries, thereby enhancing the transparency and consistency of the analysis (Friedman, 2012).

Mediation effects

To account for inconsistencies arising from the use of different standard error estimation methods, the bootstrapping approach was employed to assess the mediation effects (Hayes, 2009). A bootstrapping analysis with 2,000 resamples was performed, using 95% confidence intervals (CIs) to examine the mediating role of BPNs in the relationship between perceived supports and engagement in chatbot-assisted language learning.

For research question 3, as presented in Table 7, the mediating effects of autonomy in the relationship between perceived supports and emotional engagement was significant (β = 0.190, p = 0.022), as was its mediating role in the link between perceived supports and agentic engagement (β = 0.148, p = 0.049). However, the mediating effects of autonomy on behavioral engagement (β = 0.142, p = 0.080) and cognitive engagement (β = 0.113, p = 0.077) did not reach statistical significance. Competence served as a significant mediating factor in all four engagement dimensions. Specifically, perceived supports indirectly predicted behavioral engagement (β = 0.145, p = .022), cognitive engagement (β = 0.149, p = .003), emotional engagement (β = 0.151, p = .007), and agentic engagement (β = 0.162, p = .002) through competence. Moreover, the mediating effect of relatedness was also significant for behavioral engagement (β = 0.203, p = .029) and agentic engagement (β = 0.150, p = .039), whereas its mediating effects on cognitive engagement (β = 0.114, p = .114) and emotional engagement (β = 0.102, p = .152) were not statistically significant.

Qualitative results

In terms of autonomy, two sub-themes were summarized from the interview data, including choosing a topic (9 references) and feeling free (8 references). A significant number of learners expressed a greater sense of independence when they had the opportunity to choose topics that intrigued them while engaging with the GenAI chatbot. For example, student 18 noted, “Due to my familiarity with this subject matter, I can manage the flow of the discussion with the chatbot.” This showcases the system’s nature of supporting autonomy, with the provision of choices viewed as a valuable strategy for enhancing engagement in accordance with the preferences of the learners. Similarly, student 14 shared, “The chatbot provides a relaxed pace, enabling me to think without any sense of urgency. I also don’t worry about making any mistakes.” This indicates that the relaxed environment allowed learners to control their own learning speed, which in turn enhanced their feeling of independence.

Regarding competence, the thematic analysis identified three sub-themes: corrective feedback (13 references), improving competence (6 references), and praise (5 references). Among the three sub-themes, corrective feedback was the most frequently mentioned factor influencing competence. For instance, Student 11 stated that “the chatbot can correct my language mistakes and provide me with the correct expressions. For example, I typed “novkov” instead of “Novikov”, and the chatbot understood and connected it to Geometry. AI really improves my English.” The second most mentioned sub-theme was improving competence. Student 13 commented, “This chatbot delivers a variety of useful recommendations for improving English skills. I am confident that my proficiency can grow with its help.” The responses reveal that the GenAI chatbot’s constructive feedback and guidance not only help learners tackle language errors but also improve their language skill development. Moreover, the third frequently mentioned was praise, as Student 4 said, “Receiving praise from the AI chatbot for my perfect grammar and superb expression makes me feel acknowledged.” Such praise recognized learners’ hard work and also supported their need for enhancing sense of accomplishment.

For relatedness, four sub-themes were identified through thematic analysis: friendly tone (2 references), using emoji (6 references), encouragement (14 references), and being understood (4 references). Encouragement emerged as the most cited factor in enhancing learners’ relatedness. Student 8 shared, “The chatbot inspires me to keep pursuing my research, particularly regarding the link between Novikov rings and quantum coherence. It feels like a friend who suggests that this work is both difficult and at the forefront of innovation.”These reactions indicate that supportive encouragement, delivered through emotionally nurturing cues, helped to create a feeling of connection. The second most mentioned sub-theme was using emoji. For instance, Student 3 indicated that “the chatbot used emoji that made me feel like I was chatting with a real person”, underscoring the role of visual cues in fostering emotional connection and simulating human-like interaction. Besides, being understood was mentioned as a factor contributing to relatedness. This was best exemplified by Student 16, who stated, “While discussing a character from Harry Potter, I mistakenly referred to them by the wrong name, yet the chatbot comprehended my message, which encouraged me to continue the conversation.” Furthermore, two students (Student 3 and Student 12) remarked on how the friendly nature of the chatbot made them feel joyful, resembling a dialogue with a close friend. The sense of being understood and the presence of a friendly attitude signify learners’ connection and address their need for relatedness, which was reinforced by perceived supports and subsequently increased their engagement.

Grounded in the SDT framework, this study developed a theoretical model to examine the relationships among perceived supports, BPNs (autonomy, competence, and relatedness), and EFL learners’ (behavioral, emotional, cognitive, and agentic) engagement in chatbot-assisted language learning. Most of the findings aligned with the proposed model and validated the hypotheses, indicating that perceived supports have significant and positive effects on students’ engagement, mediated by BPNs. The specific results are discussed in the rest of this section.

Research question 1 examined the impact of perceived supports on BPNs in chatbot-assisted language learning. The results indicated that perceived supports significantly and positively predicted EFL learners’ BPNs, suggesting that when learners perceive supports from GenAI chatbots, their BPNs are more likely to be fulfilled. These findings align with previous studies (Chiu et al., 2022; Wang & Wang, 2024; Xia et al., 2024), which highlight the role of supportive learning contexts in fostering BPNs (Ryan & Deci, 2000). The positive influence of perceived supports on BPNs may be attributed to key features of GenAI chatbots, such as fostering relatedness through encouragement and emojis (Huang et al., 2019), promoting autonomy by offering choices (Chiu, 2021), and enhancing competence by providing personalized feedback (Li, 2023, 2025; Wu & Li, 2024a). Regarding the impact of perceived supports on EFL learners’ engagement, the results indicate that perceived support significantly and positively influences cognitive and emotional engagement. This finding is consistent with Zhan and Yan (2025), who noted that learners perceive GenAI chatbots as effective scaffolds for delivering feedback and recognizing learners’ emotions, thereby fostering both cognitive and emotional engagement. However, contrasting previous research (e.g. Khurma et al., 2024; Wang & Xue, 2024), perceived supports showed no significant direct effects on behavioral or agentic engagement. One possible explanation is that these two dimensions of engagement may rely more heavily on intrinsic motivation, which is primarily driven by the satisfaction of BPNs rather than external support (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Fang et al., 2019). Beyond this motivational explanation, task-related or contextual factors may also have contributed to the non-significant results. For instance, the chatbot’s limited agentic functionalities or learners’ limited proficiency in using GenAI tools may have moderated the effects of perceived supports. Jeon (2024) similarly found that limited proficiency in using GenAI systems reduced learners’ perceived control and hindered behavioral engagement.

Research question 2 focused on the effects of BPNs on engagement. The results showed that BPNs positively influenced engagement, consistent with the SDT framework, which posits that they serve as the foundation for engagement (Ryan & Deci, 2017, 2020). Specifically, the positive effect of autonomy on engagement highlights the importance of self-directed learning opportunities (Zhou et al., 2019). For instance, those who experience a feeling of autonomy in their language learning are more likely to invest their energy and focus on educational pursuits (Chiu, 2021). The significant impact of competence on engagement highlights its critical role in fostering active participation and deep learning (Shi et al., 2024). An explanation could be that learners who see themselves as skilled are more inclined to dedicate effort and adopt strategic techniques, such as analytical thinking and evaluation, to address language learning challenges (Gao et al., 2023). Additionally, relatedness plays a crucial role in influencing engagement, highlighting the importance of meaningful and emotionally supportive interactions in EFL learning (Xia et al., 2023). This finding resonates with Duong et al. (2023), who argued that encouraging social interactions and emotional backing in AI-supported education can alleviate learners’ anxiety and cultivate a cooperative learning environment, which in turn boosts engagement.

Research question 3 addressed the mediating effects of BPNs on the relationship between perceived supports and engagement. The results revealed different mediating roles of autonomy, competence, and relatedness across the four dimensions of engagement. Specifically, autonomy mediated the relationships between perceived supports and both emotional and agentic engagement, but not behavioral or cognitive engagement, consistent with Reeve (2013), who emphasized that autonomy facilitates intrinsic motivation and proactive involvement in learning. The lack of sufficient interaction time and inadequate organization of tasks could account for the shortcomings in effective mediation regarding both behavioral and cognitive engagement, which may hinder learners’ continuous efforts and their ability to engage in deeper cognitive processes (Jang et al., 2010). Implications

This study offers three key theoretical contributions, yielding a more nuanced understanding of learner engagement in chatbot-assisted language learning. First, it enhances the existing SDT framework by factoring in perceived supports, showcasing its contribution to addressing learners’ BPNs and providing valuable information on how GenAI chatbots can promote learner engagement. Second, while earlier research has utilized the SDT in tech-enhanced educational settings (Li et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2023), it places the framework within the context of chatbot-assisted language learning among Chinese EFL learners, thus validating its relevance and promoting its wider use in language learning supported by GenAI. Third, in contrast to research that treats BPNs and engagement as composite variables (Chen & Jang, 2010; Hsu et al., 2019), it offers a differentiated account of how each psychological needs individually contributes to the four dimensions of engagement (Shi et al., 2024).

This study has practical relevance for teachers, designers, and researchers alike. First, teachers may integrate GenAI chatbots to create more engaging, personalized, and interactive learning experiences (Kohnke et al., 2023; Lo et al., 2024). For instance, GenAI chatbots can be used to provide immediate language feedback during out-of-class learning tasks, thereby reinforcing learners’ competence and encouraging autonomous practice (Guo et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2024). Second, designers should incorporate autonomy-supportive features (e.g. offering choices in the learning process), competence-enhancing feedback, and elements that promote relatedness (e.g. the use of emojis to create a positive atmosphere) into the functionalities of GenAI chatbots (Chiu, 2022). For example, multimodal cues such as emojis or emotional icons can be incorporated to convey encouragement and foster a stronger sense of social connection. Third, researchers should continue to explore how GenAI chatbot-assisted language learning can be optimized across diverse learner profiles and instructional contexts, as well as examine its long-term impact on language development (Wu & Li, 2024b). For instance, future studies may compare the effects of different GenAI chatbots across proficiency levels or motivational profiles in both classroom-based and self-directed settings.

Conclusion

This study adopted the SDT as the guiding theoretical framework to examine the effects of perceived supports on EFL learners’ engagement in chatbot-assisted language learning, with BPNs serving as a mediating factor. The findings indicated that perceived supports significantly enhanced cognitive and emotional engagement, but did not directly influence behavioral or agentic engagement. In addition, perceived supports strongly predicted learners’ BPNs – autonomy, competence, and relatedness – which, in turn, positively influenced all four dimensions of engagement. Autonomy mediated the relationships between perceived supports and both emotional and agentic engagement, but not behavioral or cognitive engagement. Competence, by contrast, served as a consistent mediator across all engagement dimensions. As for relatedness, its mediating role was limited to behavioral and agentic engagement, with no significant indirect effects observed for cognitive or emotional engagement.
