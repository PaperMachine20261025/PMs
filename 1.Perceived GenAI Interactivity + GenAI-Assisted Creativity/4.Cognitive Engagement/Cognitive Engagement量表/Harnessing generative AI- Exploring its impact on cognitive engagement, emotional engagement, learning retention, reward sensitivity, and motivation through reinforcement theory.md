With the rapid integration of artificial intelligence into educational environments, understanding its influence on student learning and engagement has become increasingly critical. This study examines the impact of generative AI on students’ cognitive engagement, emotional engagement, learning retention, reward sensitivity, and motivational engagement through the lens of Rein­ forcement Theory. A total of 487 undergraduate students from seven universities in North China participated in the study. The study employed SPSS and AMOS for descriptive statistics, corre­ lation analysis, regression analysis, and structural equation modeling (SEM) to explore the complex relationships between generative AI and student outcomes. The findings revealed a significant positive relationship between generative AI and all measured student outcomes. Specifically, 62 % of the variance in cognitive engagement, 66 % in emotional engagement, 46 % in learning retention, 50 % in reward sensitivity, and 72 % in motivation were attributed to the influence of AI-driven learning tools. These results highlight the role of AI in enhancing cognitive processing, fostering emotional connections with learning materials, improving retention through personalized learning, and strengthening reward-based motivation. The study further confirms that students’ engagement with generative AI is a strong predictor of their learning outcomes. Frequent interaction with AI-generated content enhances cognitive comprehension, emotional investment, and adaptive learning behaviors, reinforcing intrinsic and extrinsic motivation. The findings underscore the transformative potential of AI-assisted learning environments in higher education, offering valuable implications for educators, policymakers, and technology developers seeking to optimize AI integration in academic settings.

In recent years, the emergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) has transformed various domains of human activity, including education, business, personal development, and language learning (Derakhshan et al., 2025; Kruk and Kału˙zna, 2025; Liu and Wang, 2024; Lu et al., 2024; Zhi and Wang, 2024). Generative AI, characterized by its ability to produce novel content—such as text, images, and even music—has revealed a profound potential to influence cognitive processes, emotional responses, learning retention, reward sensitivity, and motivational engagement. These dynamics are particularly relevant in an educational setting, where

Learning retention refers to the ability to recall and apply knowledge over time, a critical component of effective education (Jin and Wang, 2019; Qi and Wang, 2024). Generative AI can support learning retention through methods such as spaced repetition, where information is revisited at intervals that align with cognitive research findings on memory. Using an AI-driven system, learners can engage with material in a way that reinforces their memory and understanding of concepts, thereby increasing retention rates (Van Ryzin & Roseth, 2021). Reinforcement Theory suggests that consistent and timely rewards for correct responses can solidify learning, making Generative AI an invaluable tool in this regard. Reward sensitivity, the degree to which individuals respond to rewards, plays a crucial role in motivation. The interactive nature of Generative AI can tailor reward strategies to individual learners, enhancing their engagement and satisfaction. For instance, AI can reward students for achieving specific learning milestones or engaging deeply with content, thereby reinforcing desired behaviors. In alignment with Reinforcement Theory, when learners receive personalized rewards, their motivation to participate in learning activities is likely to increase (Crawford et al., 2024; Pan et al., 2025). Finally, motivational engagement encapsulates the desire and enthusiasm to participate in academic activities, which is vital for successful learning out­ comes. Generative AI can enhance motivational engagement by creating immersive and adaptive learning environments. When stu­ dents perceive AI as a supportive tool that responds to their needs and interests, their intrinsic motivation is likely to flourish. Reinforcement Theory emphasizes that the provision of meaningful rewards—whether through enhanced understanding, immediate feedback, or a sense of accomplishment—can significantly impact students’ willingness to engage with learning materials (Ding and Wang, 2025; Li and Pan, 2025).

The study highlights ongoing concerns about integrating AI in education despite promising findings. While AI technologies offer innovative solutions to enhance learning experiences, several critical issues remain unresolved. One major concern is the unequal access to AI tools among students from diverse socioeconomic backgrounds. Learners from underprivileged communities may lack the necessary digital infrastructure—such as high-speed internet, updated devices, or technical support—which can limit their ability to benefit from AI-enhanced learning environments. This digital divide risks reinforcing existing educational inequalities, allowing only more affluent students to gain advantages from personalized AI support (Ho et al., 2024). Another pressing issue is the potential decline in critical thinking skills that may result from over-reliance on AI-generated content. When learners are frequently presented with ready-made answers or suggestions, they may engage less in analytical thinking, independent problem-solving, or creative exploration. This dependency on AI could reduce opportunities for students to build essential cognitive skills that are vital for academic and real-world success. In this context, educators and researchers express concern about whether these technologies foster meaningful learning or simply encourage surface-level interaction with educational materials (Zhang et al., 2024).

The study underscores the critical importance of leveraging Generative AI as a transformative tool to enhance learners’ cognitive and emotional engagement, thereby enabling more effective and responsive teaching practices. By utilizing AI-driven insights and capabilities, educators can tailor instruction to individual student needs, fostering personalized learning experiences that deepen understanding, increase academic achievement, and support student-centered pedagogy. However, alongside these pedagogical ad­ vancements, the integration of AI into educational environments necessitates careful consideration of a range of ethical concerns.

2. Literature review

2.1. Theoretical framework

Reinforcement Theory, initially developed by B.F. Skinner in the context of behavioral psychology, asserts that behavior is shaped and influenced by the consequences that follow it. In educational settings, this theory posits that positive outcomes (rewards) following specific behaviors will increase the likelihood of those behaviors being repeated, while negative outcomes (punishments) will decrease the likelihood of those behaviors (Chung and Cheon, 2020; Pan and Wang, 2025; Wang et al., 2024). This principle is particularly relevant when examining the interaction between learners and Generative AI, as feedback and reinforcement can significantly in­ fluence cognitive engagement, emotional responses, and overall motivation. Cognitive processes encompass a range of functions, including perception, memory, and decision-making. Generative AI has the potential to transform these cognitive processes in educational contexts by providing personalized and adaptive content that caters to students’ individual learning styles ( He et al., 2025). Generative AI can tailor educational content based on student performance and preferences, fostering deeper cognitive engagement. According to Reinforcement Theory, when learners receive immediate, positive feedback for engaging with tailored content, they are likely to enhance their cognitive effort in future learning tasks (Derakhshan and Azari Noughabi, 2024; Stefansson et al., 2016).

Generative AI systems can offer real-time feedback, allowing learners to adjust their approaches to problem-solving (Ho et al., 2024). This aligns with the concept of reinforcement, where constructive feedback acts as a reward that encourages persistence and effort. Emotional responses significantly influence cognitive processing and learning outcomes. When learners interact with Gener­ ative AI, their emotional states can change based on the quality of feedback received and the success achieved in learning tasks (Zhang et al., 2024). According to Reinforcement Theory, positive emotional responses to AI-generated feedback, such as enjoyment and satisfaction, can motivate students to engage more deeply with the material. When learners experience positive emotions, their attentiveness and willingness to explore further are increased. If an AI system provides feedback that elicits frustration or discour­ agement, it may hinder engagement (McCormick et al., 2016).

2.2. Related studies

Understanding emotional dynamics enables educators to design AI interactions that minimize negative emotional responses while maximizing positive reinforcement (Lippolis et al., 2024). Learning retention is fundamental for effective education, as it determines how well learners can recall and apply knowledge over time. Generative AI can play a significant role in enhancing retention through reinforcement mechanisms. AI can identify and reward learners when they achieve specific milestones or correctly apply concepts, reinforcing retention and promoting further exploration of the content (Jin & Wang, 2019). Reward sensitivity refers to an individual’s responsiveness to reinforcement, which directly affects motivation and behavioral engagement. Generative AI applications can enhance reward sensitivity in several ways. With AI’s ability to analyze individual learning patterns, educators can design personalized reward systems that motivate each learner according to their unique preferences. Tailoring rewards to specific achievements increases the likelihood that students will respond positively, thus reinforcing desired behaviors (Ho et al., 2024). AI systems can enhance both intrinsic motivation (internal satisfaction derived from learning) and extrinsic motivation (external rewards) by offering rewarding experiences. The balance of these motivations can contribute to sustained engagement (Derakhshan and Yin, 2024; Shi, 2025).

The empirical studies provide substantial evidence of the positive relationships between Generative AI and cognitive processes, emotional responses, learning retention, reward sensitivity, and motivational engagement (Broeren et al., 2024; Kruk and Kału˙zna, 2025; Pogorskiy and Beckmann, 2023; Yi et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024). They reinforce the notion that effective interaction with AI systems can be enhanced through principles of Reinforcement Theory, where positive outcomes and tailored feedback foster engaging and meaningful learning experiences. As research in this area continues to evolve, future studies should aim to explore long-term outcomes and the potential for integrating Generative AI into diverse educational contexts, ultimately informing best practices for leveraging technology to enhance learning (Derakhshan and Zhang, 2024; He et al., 2025).

3. Method

3.1. Participants

Participants (N = 493) in the present study were selected from 7 colleges and universities in North China, but 6 of them were invalid (2 were due to the informed consent forms not being agreed upon, and 4 had issues with the age section: one filled in a name, another filled in a word “male”, other four filled in “1”, “2”, “56” and “68” respectively). The sample comprised 314 males and 173 females aged 15–25 years. In terms of learning experience, most participants were freshmen. Participants were from different majors, including Engineering (286), Science (120), Humanities (76) and Medical Science (5). Detailed demographic information is presented below (Table 1).

Cognitive Engagement in Learning with AI Questionnaire (CELAIQ) The questionnaire is designed to measure learners’ cognitive engagement when using AI-based educational tools (Liang et al., 2023). The questionnaire consists of 10 items categorized into three key components: attention and focus, critical thinking and problem-solving, and self-regulation and persistence. The questionnaire is developed based on established cognitive engagement frameworks and AI-based learning theories. Expert reviews ensure the clarity and relevance of items to the constructs measured. Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) using AMOS or SPSS verifies factor loadings, with items loading above 0.50 indicating strong construct validity. Cronbach’s alpha (α) values for each subscale should exceed 0.70, indicating strong reliability: Attention and Focus: 0.78–0.85, Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving: 0.80–0.88, and Self-Regulation and Persistence: 0.82–0.90.

3.2.3. Emotional Response to AI-Assisted Learning Questionnaire (ERAIL) The questionnaire is designed to assess learners’ emotional reactions when engaging with AI-based educational tools (Shi, 2025). The questionnaire consists of 15 items, categorized into four key components: positive emotions, negative emotions, emotional regulation, and perceived AI support. It is a 5-point Likert-type response format (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree) to assess participants’ agreement with each statement. The questionnaire is developed based on established theories of emotional responses and AI-assisted learning, ensuring all items are relevant and representative. Expert reviews are conducted to refine wording and ensure alignment with emotional response constructs. Cronbach’s alpha (α) values for each subscale should exceed 0.70, indicating strong reliability: Positive Emotions: 0.80–0.88, Negative Emotions: 0.78–0.85, Emotional Regulation: 0.82–0.89, and Perceived AI Support: 0.76–0.84.

3.2.4. Learning Retention in AI-Enhanced Environments (LRAIE) The questionnaire is designed to assess learners’ ability to retain knowledge and skills gained through AI-assisted learning expe­ riences (Alshahrani & Qureshi, 2024). The questionnaire consists of 10 items, categorized into three main components: knowledge retention, skill retention, and long-term learning engagement. It is a 5-point Likert-type response format (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree) to assess participants’ agreement with each statement. Developed based on existing theories of learning retention and AI-enhanced education. Items were reviewed by experts in educational technology and cognitive science to ensure alignment with learning retention constructs. Convergent validity is confirmed if Average Variance Extracted (AVE) > 0.50 for each component. Discriminant validity is established if the AVE values exceed the squared correlations between factors. Cronbach’s alpha (α) values indicate high reliability for each subscale: Knowledge Retention: 0.81–0.88, Skill Retention: 0.79–0.86, and Long-Term Learning Engagement: 0.82–0.89.

3.2.5. Reward Sensitivity in AI-Assisted Learning Questionnaire (RSAIQ) The questionnaire is a tool designed to assess how learners respond to reinforcement (both positive and negative) within the context of AI-assisted learning environments (Buçinca et al., 2024). The questionnaire is structured into three distinct components, each targeting a different aspect of reward sensitivity that can influence learning behaviors, motivation, and engagement in such settings. It is a 5-point Likert-type response format (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree) to assess participants’ agreement with each statement. The RSAIQ offers insights into the emotional and cognitive responses of learners to different types of reinforcement in AI-based learning settings. By identifying these sensitivities, educators and AI developers can tailor educational interventions to optimize learner engagement and performance. Items were reviewed by experts in educational technology and cognitive science to ensure alignment with reward sensitivity constructs. Internal consistency can be assessed using Cronbach’s alpha. A high alpha co­ efficient (α =.89) suggests that the items within each component are measuring the same underlying construct and are internally consistent.

3.2.6. Motivational Engagement in AI-Enhanced Learning (MEAIEL) The questionnaire is designed to assess various motivational factors and levels of engagement in students participating in AI- enhanced learning environments (Jia & Tu, 2024). It is a 5-point Likert-type response format (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree) to assess participants’ agreement with each statement. It explores how both intrinsic and extrinsic motivations, as well as direct interaction with AI tools, influence students’ learning behaviors and their overall engagement in AI-mediated education. The questionnaire consists of three main components: Intrinsic Motivation, Extrinsic Motivation, and Engagement with AI Tools. Expert reviews are conducted to refine wording and ensure alignment with motivation constructs. A Cronbach’s alpha value above 0.70 for the questionnaire (α =.91) indicates good internal consistency, meaning that the items within each component are measuring the same underlying construct.

A consent letter was first distributed to participants, clearly outlining the voluntary nature of their participation and providing assurances regarding confidentiality and anonymity. The letter explicitly stated that participants had the right to withdraw at any stage without any negative consequences. Additionally, it included a brief explanation of the study’s objectives, procedures, and the ex­ pected duration of participation. To ensure the relevance and validity of the study, specific inclusion criteria were established. Only university teachers who had experience in technology-assisted teaching were invited to participate. Furthermore, participants needed to be actively teaching at the time of data collection, as their responses were expected to reflect real-time perceptions and practices. Those who did not meet these criteria were excluded from the study. The consent form detailed the study’s purpose, the nature of participation, potential risks, and benefits. It emphasized that all data would be used solely for research purposes and that no personally identifiable information would be disclosed. Participants were required to read and sign the form electronically before proceeding, ensuring that they fully understood the research process and their rights. To maintain linguistic and conceptual equiv­ alence, the research instruments, originally in English, underwent a rigorous back translation process. First, a bilingual expert translated the scale into Chinese. Another independent translator, unfamiliar with the original text, then translated it back into English. This process ensured that the translated version retained the same meaning as the original, reducing potential misinterpretations or cultural biases. Any discrepancies between the original and back-translated versions were carefully reviewed and resolved by a panel of experts. Strict measures were taken to protect participants’ confidentiality and anonymity. The study complied with data protection regulations, ensuring that all collected responses remained secure and accessible only to the research team. Identifiable information was not linked to responses, and data were stored in encrypted files. Additionally, access was restricted to authorized personnel only, preventing unauthorized use or disclosure. After obtaining consent, the researchers addressed any potential issues related to the scale, including clarity, wording, and cultural relevance. Once these concerns were resolved, the questionnaire was distributed via Wen­ juanxing, a widely used online survey platform in China. The platform facilitated the efficient collection of responses while main­ taining confidentiality. A total of 487 valid responses were obtained, ensuring a robust dataset for analysis. By implementing these rigorous ethical and methodological safeguards, the study upheld high research standards, ensuring that the findings were both reliable and ethically sound.

4.1. Measurement model testing

The researchers used Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) to evaluate the accuracy of the surveys and the relationships between the variables. The results of the CFA confirmed that the observed variables aligned well with the theoretical constructs, validating both their uniqueness and interconnections. Convergent validity was assessed through strong factor loadings, while discriminant validity was examined by comparing the average variance extracted (AVE) with the squared correlations between constructs. The analysis confirmed that the constructs were distinct when the AVE exceeded the squared correlations. Overall, CFA provided valuable insights into the relationships among the variables, with the findings presented in the following tables and figures, further supporting the validity of the constructs.

Table 2 presents the descriptive statistics for 487 participants across multiple variables related to their engagement and experiences in AI-assisted learning environments. The Mean values across all variables indicate that, on average, participants report a generally positive experience with AI-assisted learning tools. However, areas such as Critical Thinking and Knowledge received somewhat lower ratings, suggesting room for improvement in fostering critical thinking skills and knowledge acquisition through AI tools. The Standard Deviations highlight the variability in responses, with some variables like Critical Thinking and Knowledge showing higher variability, indicating that participants’ experiences may differ significantly in these areas. Skewness results suggest that most variables are rated more positively, but some (e.g., Critical Thinking and Extrinsic Motivation) have a slight tendency towards more negative responses. Kurtosis values indicate that the distributions of responses are generally close to normal, with most participants clustering around the middle of the scale, but some variables show slight variations in response distribution.

Fig. 1 presents a model illustrating the relationships between the variables. Perceived usefulness (PU) and ease of use (PEOU) significantly impact motivation, suggesting that students who find technology helpful and easy to use are more motivated. Technology acceptance enhances cognitive engagement, implying that AI-driven tools support students’ attention, critical thinking, and selfregulation. The more students accept the technology, the stronger their emotional responses, including positive and negative emo­ tions. Motivation strongly predicts reward sensitivity, affecting intrinsic/extrinsic rewards, reinforcement, and punishment sensitivity. Cognitive engagement is crucial for knowledge, skill, and long-term retention, suggesting that higher engagement leads to better learning outcomes. Emotional responses significantly influence learning retention, reinforcing the role of emotional regulation in effective learning. AI support positively impacts students’ emotional regulation and overall learning experience. The influence of reward sensitivity (intrinsic/extrinsic rewards, punishment, and reinforcement) on learning retention suggests that students who are more responsive to rewards tend to retain information better.

5. Discussion

The study sought to determine whether there is a significant relationship between generative AI, cognitive engagement, emotional engagement, learning retention, reward sensitivity, and motivational engagement. The findings indicate a significant positive rela­ tionship among these variables. Specifically, the integration of generative AI tools not only enhances students’ cognitive processing but also fosters a deeper emotional connection with the learning materials. These results are in line with Derakhshan’s (2025) study highlighting the role of emotional engagement and goal orientation through generative AI. Additionally, personalized and adaptive learning experiences facilitated by generative AI contribute to improved retention. Furthermore, students who exhibit higher reward sensitivity show increased levels of engagement and motivation when interacting with AI-driven learning environments. The second research question examined the extent to which students’ use of generative AI predicts their cognitive and emotional engagement, learning retention, reward sensitivity, and motivational engagement. The results revealed that frequent utilization of generative AI is a strong predictor of these outcomes. Specifically, consistent interaction with AI-generated content significantly enhances cognitive understanding and emotional investment, leading to better long-term retention of information. Additionally, AI-assisted learning establishes an adaptive reward system that reinforces both intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, promoting sustained academic engagement and improvements in performance.

The findings underscore the transformative potential of generative AI in educational settings. The significant positive relationships revealed between generative AI and various facets of student experience indicate that technological integration in the classroom can profoundly impact how students engage cognitively and emotionally with content. The evidence suggests that generative AI tools facilitate deeper cognitive processing by providing tailored resources that meet individual learning needs. This personalization aligns with cognitive theories that posit learners are more engaged when content is relevant and adaptive to their levels of understanding. Such engagement is crucial for the acquisition of complex concepts and skills, leading to improved academic performance. The emotional connection students feel towards learning materials, enhanced by generative AI, is particularly noteworthy. By personal­ izing learning experiences and providing real-time feedback, generative AI tools can elicit positive emotional responses, such as cu­ riosity and satisfaction. This emotional engagement has been shown to play a significant role in the learning process, as it can enhance motivation and deepen investment in educational tasks. The improved retention rates linked to generative AI applications can be attributed to the effectiveness of adaptive learning environments where students receive content in a way that reinforces prior knowledge.

Furthermore, these findings confirm the principles of Reinforcement Theory by validating how AI-enhanced educational tools shape learning behaviors. This theory highlights the role of rewards and feedback in fostering or diminishing motivation and engagement. For instance, positive reinforcement provided by AI tools—such as consistent and timely rewards for correct respon­ ses—can solidify learning. Additionally, the findings emphasize the role of reward sensitivity in fostering engagement, particularly in AI-driven environments. Students who are sensitive to rewards are more likely to internalize adaptive rewards provided by generative AI, thereby sustaining cycles of effort and persistence. Crucially, generative AI’s ability to tailor reinforcement mechanisms bridges intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, aligning with the core principle of Reinforcement Theory: behavior is shaped by its consequences. The combination of AI and Reinforcement Theory represents a paradigm shift in education, demonstrating how well-timed rewards can transform short-term learning moments into lasting knowledge and skills.

6. Conclusion

The integration of Generative AI into educational contexts presents a transformative opportunity to enhance various aspects of the learning experience, including cognitive engagement, emotional involvement, learning retention, reward sensitivity, and motivational engagement. This study has demonstrated significant positive relationships between generative AI and these psychological constructs, offering empirical evidence that underscores the potential of technology to deepen students’ engagement and improve academic outcomes. The findings reveal that generative AI tools can personalize educational experiences, enriching cognitive processes and fostering emotional connections with learning materials. Additionally, consistent interaction with AI-generated content not only enhances students’ understanding and retention of knowledge but also supports an adaptive reward system that cultivates both intrinsic and extrinsic motivation. By illustrating how generative AI can serve as a strong predictor of engagement and performance, this research contributes valuable insights to the field of educational technology. As educational institutions increasingly adopt these technologies, it is essential to consider the implications of these findings for pedagogical practices, policy development, and future research. Emphasizing the balance between the benefits of generative AI and the associated challenges, such as data privacy, equity of access, and the evolving role of educators, will be crucial for effectively harnessing technology’s potential in education.

6.2. Limitations

While this study provides valuable insights into the relationship between Generative AI and cognitive, emotional, learning retention, reward sensitivity, and motivational engagement, several limitations must be acknowledged. The study may have been conducted with a limited sample size or specific demographic groups, which could impact the generalizability of the findings. A more diverse sample across different age groups, educational levels, and cultural backgrounds is needed to enhance applicability. The research primarily explored short-term outcomes associated with interactions with generative AI. Longitudinal studies are necessary to understand the long-term impacts of such technology on learning retention, engagement, and performance over time. The findings may be influenced by the specific generative AI tools or platforms utilized in the study. Different tools may yield varying impacts based on their design, functionality, and the contexts in which they are implemented. Future studies should investigate a range of generative AI applications to draw more nuanced conclusions. The study may not have fully accounted for contextual factors, such as classroom environment, teacher engagement, or the presence of supportive resources. These variables can significantly influence the effec­ tiveness of generative AI interventions and should be included in future research designs.

6.3. Future studies

Future studies should adopt longitudinal designs to examine how the relationships between generative AI and various learning outcomes evolve over time. This approach can provide insights into the sustainability of cognitive engagement, emotional investment, retention, and motivation. Expanding the sample size and including diverse populations will enhance the generalizability of findings. Future research should consider including participants from various educational backgrounds, age groups, and cultural contexts to gain a comprehensive understanding of the effects of generative AI. Employing a mixed-methods research design that combines qualitative and quantitative approaches can provide a more holistic understanding of how generative AI impacts learning experiences. Interviews, focus groups, and observational data can complement quantitative measures and offer rich contextual insights. Conducting comparative studies that evaluate different generative AI tools against traditional educational techniques or other educational tech­ nologies can help clarify the unique contributions of generative AI. This will elucidate which features or approaches are most effective in enhancing learner outcomes.
