Background: Fostering creativity in nursing education is essential for equipping students with critical thinking and problem-solving skills. Nurse educators play a pivotal role in nurturing creativity among nursing students, yet their effectiveness is shaped by institutional and technological influences. While an evidence-based practice (EBP) climate provides a structured framework for innovative teaching, the increasing integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in education necessitates that educators develop AI competence self-efficacy. However, the extent to which AI competence self-efficacy strengthens the relationship between EBP climate and creativity-nurturing behaviors remains underexplored. 2. Background

Creativity-nurturing behaviors are deliberate actions by educators to foster innovation, critical thinking, and problem-solving among stu­ dents, essential for preparing them to address complex healthcare challenges. These behaviors involve promoting student autonomy, engaging in reflective practices, integrating collaborative learning, and encouraging exploration of diverse perspectives (Brauer et al., 2024). Various dimensions of creativity-nurturing behaviors have been explored in the literature. Among these, reflective practices play a crucial role in shaping students' ability to think critically and apply creative solutions in clinical settings. (Zarpullayev et al., 2024).

Reflective practice is a structured process that enables educators and students to critically evaluate experiences, refine decision-making, and enhance problem-solving (Rojo et al., 2023). It fosters transformative learning, cognitive engagement, and adaptability in clinical settings. Strategies such as structured debriefing, guided journaling, and experi­ ential learning encourage critical analysis, challenge assumptions, and support alternative problem-solving approaches (Amin et al., 2025). Within an evidence-based practice (EBP) climate, reflection serves as a bridge between theory and clinical application, strengthening creativitynurturing behaviors in nursing education (Zarpullayev et al., 2024).

Liu (2022) demonstrated that nursing students exposed to creativityfocused teaching exhibited higher levels of innovative thinking and adaptability in clinical practice. AI competence self-efficacy refers to the confidence educators have in their ability to utilize AI technologies effectively in their teaching (Kim and Kwon, 2023). 2.1. Significance of the study and research gap

Nursing education faces increasing challenges in preparing students to meet the demands of a rapidly evolving healthcare environment. Creativity-nurturing behaviors among nurse educators are critical for fostering students' critical thinking, problem-solving, and adaptability (Asal et al., 2024). However, the integration of such behaviors is often hindered by limited institutional support and reliance on traditional teaching methods (Ehrhart et al., 2014). While EBP climate within educational institutions encourages innovative, research-informed teaching strategies, its potential remains underutilized (Duff et al., 2020). The increasing integration of AI technologies in education pre­ sents an opportunity to enhance creativity-nurturing behaviors, yet many nurse educators lack the confidence and competence to effectively incorporate these tools into their teaching practices (Nielsen et al., 2024). This gap jeopardizes the potential synergy between EBP climate, AI technologies, and creativity in nursing education.

Although existing literature emphasizes the importance of EBP climate in fostering innovation and creativity in educational settings (Liu et al., 2020; Herrman, 2024), limited research has explored its relationship with creativity-nurturing behaviors among nurse educators. Furthermore, while the role of AI in education has been studied exten­ sively, there is little evidence on how AI competence self-efficacy in­ fluences educators' ability to integrate these tools into creative, evidence-based teaching practices. The moderating role of AI compe­ tence self-efficacy in the relationship between EBP climate and creativity-nurturing behaviors remains largely unexplored. This study aims to fill this gap by examining the interplay between these variables and providing actionable insights to enhance nursing education in a technology-driven era.

2.2. Theoretical underpinnings and conceptualization of the study

This study is grounded in Bandura's social cognitive theory (SCT), which highlights the pivotal role of self-efficacy in shaping behaviors and outcomes. In the context of this study, AI competence self-efficacy among nurse educators enables them to adopt innovative teaching strategies, amplifying creativity-nurturing behaviors within an EBP climate (Yao and Wang, 2024). SCT asserts that individuals with higher self-efficacy are more likely to engage in challenging tasks, persist in overcoming barriers, and achieve desired goals, aligning with the need for educators to integrate AI into creative and evidence-informed ped­ agogies (Bogn´ar et al., 2024).

Additionally, the unified theory of acceptance and use of technology (UTAUT) identifies four key constructs—performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions—that influence individuals' intention to adopt and use technology. In the context of our study, AI competence self-efficacy aligns with performance expectancy (the belief that using AI will enhance teaching performance) and effort expectancy (the perceived ease of using AI tools). A strong EBP climate provides the facilitating conditions, such as organizational support and access to resources, that encourage educators to adopt AI technologies (Zaim et al., 2024).

Additionally, the technological pedagogical content knowledge framework examines how educators integrate technology into teaching by considering the interplay of three knowledge domains: technological knowledge, pedagogical knowledge, and content knowledge (Archambault and Barnett, 2010). In this study, AI competence selfefficacy aligns with technological knowledge, while creativitynurturing behaviors reflect the innovative use of pedagogical knowl­ edge within an evidence-based content framework.

The study integrates the principles of SCT and UTAUT into its research framework by examining how EBP climate, AI competence selfefficacy, and their interaction shape creativity-nurturing behaviors. This alignment forms the basis for the study objectives and hypotheses:

Furthermore, the model highlights the moderating role of AI competence self-efficacy (H4), demonstrating how greater confidence in AI integration enhances the positive effect of an EBP climate on creativity-nurturing behaviors.

2.4. Research question

Guided by the study framework, the study seeks to address the following research question: Does AI competence self-efficacy enhance the positive effect of EBP climate on creativity-nurturing behaviors among nurse educators?

3. Methods

3.1. Research design and setting

This study employed a descriptive, cross-sectional design with moderation analysis. The research was carried out at Egypt and Saudia Arabia with 15 nursing colleges participating in this study. This study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines (Von Elm et al., 2007).

3.2. Study participants and sample size determination

The study sample consisted of nurse educators actively teaching in the 2023–2024 academic year. Eligibility requirements included having at least one year of teaching experience in nursing education and being actively involved in teaching, while excluding those in administrative or non-teaching roles.

Sample size determination was conducted using G*Power software (version 3.1.9.7) for linear regression analysis (Faul et al., 2007), with a target statistical power of 85 % and a Type I error rate (α) of 0.05. Given the exploratory nature of this study and limited prior research on the relationships among the variables, a small effect size was selected to prevent underpowering, in line with power calculation guidelines (Dienes, 2014). This choice ensures the study's ability to detect subtle relationships, particularly when recruitment is feasible. Therefore, the minimum required sample size was calculated to be 414 nurse educa­ tors. However, to account for potential response biases, the sample size

was increased by 20 % as recommended by (Bujang, 2021), resulting in a target convenience sample of 500 participants.

3.3. Instruments

Data were collected using three scales, along with nine additional questions designed to gather information on nurse educators' de­ mographic and professional characteristics.

The Creativity Nurturing Behavior Scale for Teachers was developed by Sharma and Sharma (2018). It is designed to assess teachers' abilities to foster creativity in their students. The scale consists of 15 items across four domains: abstraction (five items), inquisitiveness (three items), motivation (three items), and critical thinking (four items). Responses are recorded on a 6-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (“completely disagree”) to 6 (“agree completely”). A higher score indicates a stronger ability to nurture creativity in students. The Cronbach's alpha for the overall scale is 0.846, indicating strong reliability (Sharma and Sharma, 2018). In this study, the Cronbach's alpha for the overall scale is 0.813 and for the four domains ranged from 0.783 to 0.82.

The Teacher AI Competence Self-Efficacy Scale was developed by Chiu et al. (2024) to assess AI competence self-efficacy among educa­ tors. It comprises 24 items across six dimensions: AI knowledge (four items), AI pedagogy (four items), AI assessment (four items), AI ethics (four items), human-centered education (four items), and professional engagement (four items). Responses are recorded on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”). Higher scores reflect higher levels of AI competence self-efficacy. The scale demonstrated strong reliability, with Cronbach's alpha values >0.87 for all dimensions (Chiu et al., 2024). In this study, the Cronbach's alpha for the overall scale is 0.802 and for the four domains ranged from 0.788 to 0.843. The School Implementation Climate Scale was developed by Thayer et al. (2022) to assess the EBP implementation climate within schools. The scale consists of 21 items distributed across seven dimensions: focus on EBP, educational support for EBP, recognition for EBP, rewards for EBP, use of data to support EBP, existing supports to deliver EBP, and EBP integration. Each dimension includes three items. Responses are recorded on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 0 (“not at all”) to 4 (“to a very great extent”). Higher scores indicate a stronger strategic implementation climate. The scale has demonstrated strong reliability, with Cronbach's alpha values ranging from 0.81 to 0.90 for individual subscales (Thayer et al., 2022). In this study, the Cronbach's alpha for the overall scale is 0.82 and for the seven dimensions ranged from 0.79 to 0.80.

3.5. Statistical analysis

Data analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS 28.0 and the PROCESS macro (v4.2). The dataset was screened for missing, multiple, or extreme responses, and five extreme responses were removed, leaving 570 valid cases for analysis, which exceed the planned sample size (see Fig. 2). Descriptive statistics summarized categorical variables as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were presented as means

To test the study hypotheses, linear regression analysis was per­ formed using Model 1 of the PROCESS macro, examining the impact of EBP climate (X) and AI competence self-efficacy (W) on creativitynurturing behaviors (Y), with their interaction also tested. Covariates, including age and training, were controlled for. The moderating role of AI competence self-efficacy was examined at three levels: low (one standard deviation below the mean), average (at the mean), and high (one standard deviation above the mean). Results were considered sig­ nificant at the 5 % significance level, with confidence intervals excluding zero.

3.6. Controlling common method variance

To assess potential Common Method Variance (CMV), Harman's onefactor test was applied. The results indicated that a single factor explained 18.25 % of the total variance, well below the 50 % threshold recommended by Aguirre-Urreta and Hu (2019). Therefore, CMV was not deemed to be a significant concern in this study.

Table 3 presents the results from univariate regression, which showed that age and prior training were significant predictors of creativity-nurturing behaviors among nurse educators. Building on this, a multiple regression analysis was conducted to further explore how EBP climate, AI competence self-efficacy, and their interaction predict creativity-nurturing behaviors, while controlling for age and prior training (see, Table 4). The results indicated that both EBP climate (β = 0.588, p < 0.001) and AI competence self-efficacy (β = 0.183, p < 0.01) positively predicted creativity-nurturing behaviors, meaning that edu­ cators who are in supportive, EBP environments and those who feel confident in their AI skills are more likely to engage in behaviors that nurture creativity in their teaching. Additionally, the interaction term (β = 0.236, p < 0.01) indicated that AI competence self-efficacy strengthens the positive relationship between EBP climate and creativity-nurturing behaviors.

The results in Table 4 also show that the regression model was sta­ tistically significant (F (75, 564) = 49.87, p < 0.001), meaning that the combination of these variables explained a substantial portion of the variation in creativity-nurturing behaviors. Specifically, the model explained 30.6 % of the variance (R2 = 0.306), indicating that around a third of the variation in these behaviors can be attributed to EBP climate, AI competence self-efficacy, and their interaction. Incorporating the interaction term increased the explanatory power of the model (ΔR2 = 0.0092, F (1, 592) = 7.511, p < 0.01), demonstrating that the combined effect of EBP climate and AI competence self-efficacy offers a more robust explanation for creativity-nurturing behaviors than either factor alone.

The interaction plot in Fig. 3 visually illustrates how the relationship between EBP climate and creativity-nurturing behaviors is influenced by levels of AI competence self-efficacy. At low levels of AI competence selfefficacy, the positive relationship between EBP climate and creativitynurturing behaviors is present, but it is relatively weaker (β = 0.50, CI [0.36, 0.62], p < 0.001). As AI competence self-efficacy increases to average levels (β = 0.59, CI [0.47, 0.70], p < 0.001) and high levels (β = 0.68, CI [0.54, 0.82], p < 0.001), the slope of the relationship steepens, reflecting a stronger positive effect. This pattern underscores that higher AI competence self-efficacy amplifies the impact of EBP climate on creativity-nurturing behaviors, emphasizing the moderating role of AI competence self-efficacy in fostering these behaviors.

5.1. EBP climate and creativity-nurturing behaviors

Our findings confirmed a significant positive relationship between EBP climate and creativity-nurturing behaviors among nurse educators, supporting H1. This result suggests that institutions fostering an EBP climate provide structured frameworks that enable nurse educators to implement innovative and evidence-based teaching strategies. The moderate effect size (R2 = 0.306) further indicates that EBP climate explains a substantial proportion of the variance in creativity-nurturing behaviors, reinforcing its importance as a key determinant.

The positive association found in this study is consistent with pre­ vious evidence indicating that EBP-oriented institutions often provide

Parallel with our findings, Jeong et al. (2024) demonstrated that tailored EBP programs foster critical thinking and innovation. Sultana et al. (2024) and Luo et al. (2024) highlighted that integrating evidencebased technologies within supportive climates boosts educators' moti­ vation and creativity. Brauer et al. (2024) emphasized interprofessional collaboration, core element in EBP climate, as vital for experimentation and innovative problem-solving.

Despite the positive impact of EBP climates, some perspectives in literature highlight potential challenges. Scott-Barrett et al. (2023) noted that disparities in institutional implementation may limit effec­ tiveness, while Duff et al. (2020) emphasized that workload pressures and resistance to change can hinder creative teaching. These viewpoints suggest that while EBP climates provide a strong foundation, addressing educator challenges and ensuring equitable access to resources is essential for maximizing their impact.

5.2. AI competence self-efficacy and creativity-nurturing behaviors

Our results revealed a positive and significant association between AI competence self-efficacy and creativity-nurturing behaviors, supporting H2. This finding indicates that nurse educators with greater confidence in using AI tools are more likely to implement technology-driven, cre­ ative teaching strategies. This aligns with social cognitive theory, which posits that self-efficacy enhances behavioral engagement and persis­ tence in complex tasks—in this case, AI-driven pedagogical innovation.

Previous research has similarly demonstrated that educators with high AI competence self-efficacy are more adept at integrating AI- powered simulations, adaptive learning platforms, and interactive dig­ ital tools to support student engagement (Kim and Kwon, 2023; Lu et al., 2024). Our findings contribute to this growing body of evidence by demonstrating that AI competence self-efficacy is not only beneficial for AI adoption but also plays a role in shaping educators' ability to nurture creativity within an EBP framework.

However, the moderate effect size suggests that while AI competence self-efficacy enhances creativity-nurturing behaviors, its influence may be contingent upon additional factors, such as organizational support, training accessibility, and personal attitudes toward technology. This aligns with prior research indicating that AI integration challenges, such as technical limitations and ethical concerns, may moderate the extent to which educators adopt AI-based innovations (Asio and Suero, 2024; Saihi et al., 2024).

5.3. EBP climate and AI competence self-efficacy

Our study also found that EBP climate was significantly associated with AI competence self-efficacy, supporting H3. This finding suggests that educators working within a structured EBP climate are more likely to develop confidence in AI-related skills, possibly due to increased exposure to institutional resources, training opportunities, and researchdriven teaching methodologies. Educators in such environments are more likely to develop technological competence and integrate AI- driven teaching methodologies (Luo et al., 2024).

These findings align with prior research demonstrating that EBP climates cultivate digital readiness. Zaim et al. (2024) found that edu­ cators in innovative EBP environments exhibit high AI adoption readi­ ness, while Zhui et al. (2024) reported a strong link between EBP climates and higher digital competence. However, contradictory

perspectives highlight challenges such as AI-related anxiety, ethical concerns, and lack of institutional support, which may reduce AI competence self-efficacy (Asio and Suero, 2024; Saihi et al., 2024). These findings emphasize the need for a balanced approach, ensuring EBP climates address barriers to AI adoption while fostering a sup­ portive environment for educators to integrate AI confidently and effectively into teaching practices.

5.4. The moderating role of AI competence self-efficacy

Our moderation analysis revealed that AI competence self-efficacy significantly moderates the relationship between EBP climate and creativity-nurturing behaviors, supporting H4. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the positive impact of EBP climate on creativity-nurturing behaviors was stronger among educators with higher AI competence self-efficacy. This result suggests that AI competence self-efficacy serves as an enabling factor, amplifying the benefits of an EBP climate by empowering edu­ cators to integrate AI tools into their teaching methods more effectively. Educators with higher AI self-efficacy may feel more confident exploring AI-driven innovations, thus enhancing their ability to implement creativity-nurturing behaviors within an EBP framework (Jeong et al., 2024). Prior research supports this finding, with studies indicating that educators who feel competent using AI tools are more likely to engage in experimental, technology-enhanced teaching strategies (Shuman et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2024b). Our findings expand on this literature by demonstrating that AI competence self-efficacy not only benefits AI adoption but also enhances educators' broader ability to translate EBP climate support into creativity-driven teaching practices.

However, the magnitude of the moderating effect (ΔR2 = 0.0092, F = 7.511) indicates that while AI competence self-efficacy plays a sta­ tistically significant role, it accounts for a relatively small proportion of variance in the relationship between EBP climate and creativitynurturing behaviors. This suggests that additional contextual factors, such as institutional policies, peer collaboration, and digital infrastruc­ ture, may further influence this relationship and warrant future exploration.

5.5. Implications of the study

Our study has significant implications for nursing education, nursing practice, policy-making, and broader health initiatives.

5.5.1. For nursing education This study emphasizes the importance of integrating EBP climate and AI competence self-efficacy into nursing education curricula. Targeted professional development programs should equip nurse educators with skills to foster creativity-nurturing behaviors using innovative AI- powered tools like simulations and digital platforms. Embedding these competencies into educational frameworks will prepare nursing stu­ dents for the rapidly evolving technological demands of healthcare, aligning their training with current and future professional expectations while enhancing critical thinking and problem-solving capabilities.

5.5.2. For nursing practice This study highlights the need for nursing practice environments to align with evidence-based and technology-driven approaches. By fostering creativity-nurturing behaviors through EBP climate and AI competence, nurse educators can produce practitioners capable of solving complex healthcare challenges. Practicing nurses should also be encouraged to adopt similar strategies, integrating AI tools and evidence-based solutions to improve patient care, enhance problemsolving, and contribute to innovative, patient-centered practices in clinical settings.

5.5.3. For policy makers and educational leadership Policy and educational leaders should invest in creating and sus­ taining robust EBP climate within nursing institutions while promoting digital literacy and AI competence. This includes providing adequate resources, regular training, and leadership support to encourage inno­ vation among educators. Policies should address potential barriers to AI adoption, such as resource inequities and resistance to change, ensuring equitable access. Leadership-driven strategies can maximize the syner­ gistic impact of EBP climate and AI competence self-efficacy on educa­ tors' creativity-nurturing behaviors.

This research contributes to understanding how organizational cli­ mates and individual competencies influence innovation in education. It highlights the broader role of EBP and AI integration in promoting creativity and innovation across sectors, encouraging interdisciplinary approaches. The findings are relevant for shaping future educational policies, workforce development, and technology-enhanced teaching frameworks globally.

5.7. Strengths and limitations

This study's strengths lie in its large and diverse sample size, enhancing the generalizability of findings, and its grounding in robust theoretical frameworks, including Bandura's social cognitive theory which strengthen its conceptual rigor. The use of validated scales en­ sures robust data collection, and different measures were employed to control bias and common method variance, ensuring validity. Addi­ tionally, the inclusion of AI competence self-efficacy as a moderating variable provides novel insights into nursing education's evolving dy­ namics. However, limitations include reliance on self-reported data, which may introduce bias, and the geographic focus on two countries may limit the applicability of results to a global context.

6. Conclusion

This study highlights the significant role of EBP climate and AI competence self-efficacy in fostering creativity-nurturing behaviors among nurse educators. These findings emphasize the necessity of fostering supportive EBP climate, enhancing AI competencies, and implementing professional development programs to prepare students for modern healthcare's technological challenges while promoting evidence-informed and creative learning environments.
