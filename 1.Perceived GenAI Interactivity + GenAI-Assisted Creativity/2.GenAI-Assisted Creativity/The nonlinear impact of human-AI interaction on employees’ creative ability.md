The nonlinear impact of human-AI interaction on employees’ creative ability

Minling Chen, Yue Zhang and Jing Xue

School of Economics and Management, Xi’an Shiyou University, Xi’an, People’s Republic of China

ABSTRACT Is AI always beneﬁcial? Despite the growing body of research on the positive outcomes of AI, some evidence still suggests the negative impact of its introduction into the workplace on employees. Taking into account the characteristics of human-AI interaction, this study seeks to answer the question of the non-linear impact of human-AI interaction on employee creativity. Utilising 284 data from China’s smart manufacturing industry, the results show that the impact of human-AI interaction on employee creativity is nonlinear, showing an increasing and then decreasing trend. Second, cognitive outsourcing plays a curve mediating role between the two, which is manifested in the fact that human-AI interaction aﬀects creativity by inducing an inverted U- shaped change in cognitive outsourcing and then aﬀecting creativity. Finally, team mastery motivational climate moderated the inverted U-shaped relationship between human-AI interaction and cognitive outsourcing. This study practically helps companies to develop appropriate human-AI interaction strategies to enhance the core competitiveness of organisations in the smart era.

1. Introduction

As enterprises increasingly adopt artiﬁcial intelligence technologies (Von Krogh 2018), human-AI interaction (HAI) has become a prevalent new mode of work within organisations (De Cremer and McGuire 2022). Voice assistants, intelligent decision-making systems, generative AI, and other common artiﬁcial intelligence technologies are transforming how employees work while placing higher demands on their creativity in areas such as machine learning and big data processing. Given that AI can increase employee labour productivity (Brynjolfsson, Mitchell, and Rock 2018), industries including healthcare, hospitality, and energy are striving to explore the positive eﬀects of HAI and thus achieve better development (Choi et al. 2020; Guo, Gu, and Huo 2025; Masudin et al. 2024).

Human-AI interaction refers to the interaction and integration process in which human beings and technological subjects (referring to AI, industrial intelligent systems, etc.) directly or indirectly share operational intentions, make joint decisions, and collaborate to accomplish tasks in an organisational environment (Gorecky et al. 2014; Romero, Stahre, and Taisch 2020). Human-AI interaction can, to a certain extent, lead to an increase in the eﬃciency of human resource management (Vrontis et al. 2022). 2. Theoretical analysis and research hypotheses

2.1. The inverted U-shape of human-AI interaction on employee creativity

The COR theory is an important motivational theory that centres on explaining how individuals protect and acquire valuable resources (Halbesleben et al. 2014). HAI empowers the sustainable development of employees’ creativity in the following ways: ﬁrst, employees enhance their knowledge integration ability with the help of HAI, which is a process of eﬀective use and reuse of knowledge resources (Zahra, Neubaum, and Hayton 2020). When employees achieve eﬀective accumulation of knowledge resources due to AI assistance, it then lays the resource foundation for the stimulation of creativity and innovative behaviour. Knowledge integration promotes organisational innovation (Chen et al. 2021; Tsou 2012), and the ability to use existing knowledge and information to generate diﬀerent combinations and reconﬁgurations is a source of innovation. Second, moderate HAI takes employees away from tedious tasks (Vrontis et al. 2022) and focuses their attention on creative tasks. And due to the change in interaction objects, employees are motivated to move from traditional learning methods to more technology-driven learning approaches (Markauskaite et al. 2022), which is more conducive to employee creativity. Finally, AI enhances the accessibility and comprehensibility of information with powerful data processing capabilities (Gibbard et al. 2025), helping employees to apply otherwise incomprehensible information to their work and improve their ability to innovate.

H1: There is an inverted U-shaped relationship between HAI and employee creativity.

2.2. The mediating role of cognitive outsourcing

Given the scarcity of cognitive resources and the complexity of processing, employees tend to engage in cognitive outsourcing behaviours when AI can solve problems faster. The human cognitive system is characterised by an extended mindset, which oﬄoads some internal activities to the environment to aid cognitive processing (Weis and Wiese 2022). ‘Knowing where information is’ rather than ‘remembering the information itself (Sparrow, Liu, and Wegner 2011).’ At low levels of HAI, employees do not yet have an indepth understanding of the AI’s capabilities, resulting in a low level of cognitive outsourcing. However, as the interaction deepens, deeper trust in the AI prompts increased cognitive outsourcing. Because most AIs have powerful and versatile problem diagnosis and solving capabilities (Guha et al. 2023), employees perceive that they can accurately perform a variety of tasks with consistent and reliable results. Secondly, AIs provide employees with suﬃcient information resources (Dwivedi et al. 2023) to help them process information and analyze data (Raisch and Krakowski 2021), and improve the eﬃciency of storing and transferring explicit knowledge (Pai et al. 2022). 4.1. Common method bias test

A number of approaches were taken to minimise the impact of common methodological biases. First, in the design of the questionnaire, well-established and validated scales were selected, and the questions were made as concise as possible and could be eﬀectively understood. Second, the questionnaire followed the code of ethics for social science research and was anonymous to minimise ethical issues and the creation of bias. Finally, all items were subjected to principal component analysis using Harman’s one-way test. Harman’s one-way test showed that after unrotated principal component analysis of all items for the four variables: human-AI interaction, cognitive outsourcing, employee creativity, and team mastery motivational climate, the ﬁrst factor obtained explained 36.45% of the variance, which is much lower than the baseline of 50%. 5. Conclusion and discussion

Based on the COR theory, this study examined the nonlinear impact mechanism of human-AI interaction on employee creativity. The main ﬁndings of the study are as follows:

First, this study conﬁrms the double-edged sword eﬀect of human-AI interaction: AI is a double-edged sword, and the principle of ‘moderation’ should be followed in order to maximise the potential of AI. Moderate interaction empowers employee creativity, while excessive interaction is a hindrance to creativity. Therefore, the results of human-AI interaction are not positive, in part echoing the controversy (Karhade and Dong 2021; Yin, Jiang, and Niu 2024) that over-reliance on AI hinders the development of employee creativity and corporate innovation. Moreover, this doubleedged sword eﬀect may be more pronounced in knowledge-intensive industries closely tied to cognitive resources, whereas its intensity may diminish for strategic decision-makers or labour-intensive positions.

Second, cognitive outsourcing plays an important mediating role between human-AI interaction and employee creativity. According to the COR theory, in human-AI interaction scenarios, moderate interaction is seen as a way for individuals to gain access to information processing eﬃciency, technical support, and

5.1. Theoretical contributions

First, it reveals the inverted U-shaped eﬀect of HAI on employee creativity. There is a theoretical debate among established studies about the consequences of AI technology application in the workplace: a part of scholars hold a single view that AI positively or negatively aﬀects employee creativity in a linear relationship (Skaria, Satam, and Khalpey 2020; Verma and Singh 2022). While another group of scholars believes that AI is a double-edged sword, for example, AI is employed to promote innovative behaviours by enhancing knowledge workers’ challenging stress assessments, while inhibiting innovative behaviours by promoting their obstructive stress assessments (Dong et al. 2025). Focusing on the dynamic process of human-AI interaction, we explored the impact of HAI on employee creativity from a nonlinear perspective, breaking through the limitations of existing studies that think linearly while enriching the study of antecedent variables of creativity.

Second, cognitive outsourcing is introduced as a mediator, thus making an important contribution to the cognitive outsourcing literature. Academic research on cognitive outsourcing has remained at the level of theoretical analysis, and empirical research has been less involved. We innovatively construct the framework of ‘human-AI interaction – cognitive outsourcing – creativity,’ which reveals the mediating mechanism of cognitive outsourcing between HAI and employee creativity. 5.2. Management enlightenment

This study presents the following practical insights. First, optimise the HAI process to stimulate employees’ creativity. Enterprises should guide employees to develop a rational perspective on human-AI interaction, establish a negative list for AI applications to deﬁne usage boundaries, and emphasise data privacy, security, and compliance. Additionally, by organising AI skills training and establishing AI innovation platforms, customised AI training programmes and certiﬁcation pathways are designed for employees across diﬀerent functions to enhance their human-AI literacy. For instance, Accenture launched the AI Navigator for Enterprise platform, which aims to systematically elevate employees’ ability to harness AI and maximise its value.

Third, it enriches and expands the research on team mastery motivational climate. Although a few studies have found a signiﬁcant relationship between team motivational climate and employee creativity and innovation outcomes (Bari et al. 2019; Zhao, Yu, and Zhang 2023), the mechanism of team motivational climate’s inﬂuence on employee creativity needs to be further clariﬁed. In this paper, starting from team-level heterogeneity, we innovatively introduced team mastery motivational climate as a moderating variable and constructed the moderated mediated eﬀect model. By validating the moderating role of team mastery motivational climate in the relationship between HAI and cognitive outsourcing, this study addresses gaps in existing research and reﬁnes the contextual mechanisms underlying employee creativity formation.

Second, correctly recognise the irreversibility of cognitive outsourcing and balance knowledge internalisation and cognitive outsourcing in practice. While enjoying the dividends of technological eﬃciency, organisations also need to ensure the continuous development of employees’ core cognitive abilities. For instance, establish task lists to clearly deﬁne the creative work employees must complete independently, preventing them from losing their ability to think autonomously and integrate knowledge due to overreliance on AI. Third, build a learning culture and incentive system centred on AI literacy, integrating it into the organisational development framework. Managers should incorporate human-AI collaboration capabilities into employee competency models and promotion evaluation systems, while providing corresponding resource support. The ‘Human-AI Collaborative Innovation Award’ may be established to recognise teams and individuals quarterly who successfully leverage AI tools to solve business challenges or demonstrate best practices in human-AI collaboration. Through dual incentives of material rewards and honorary recognition, this initiative fosters an organisational culture of proactive learning and proﬁcient AI utilisation – empowering employees to master AI without becoming dependent on it.

Fourthly, organisations should dynamically monitor employees’ levels of human-AI interaction to prevent eﬀective empowerment from escalating into cognitive overload. Enterprises may systematically track employees’ usage intensity and emotional experiences during human-AI collaboration through methods such as behavioural logging and quantitative assessments. Implement diﬀerentiated management in task allocation and training: assign more innovative and hands-on work tasks to employees exhibiting excessive human-AI interaction, thereby enhancing their selfdirected creativity.

5.3. Limitations and future research direction

Despite the theoretical and practical contributions of this paper, there are still limitations, which provide opportunities for future research. First, the measurement of HAI is yet to be further explored, and future research can be expanded in terms of frequency and depth. Secondly, human-AI interaction is a dynamic process, and questionnaire surveys may introduce bias. Future research could adopt longitudinal designs or scenario studies to more eﬀectively evaluate temporal dynamics. Finally, the empirical data for this study primarily originates from Chinese employees, limiting the generalizability of its ﬁndings. Future research should collect cross-cultural data spanning multiple industries in regions such as Europe and the United States.
