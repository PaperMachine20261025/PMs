Strengthening strategizing and startup entrepreneurial readiness among Thai Gen Z management students: Do AI literacy, creativity, and seizing skills matter?

A B S T R A C T

Keywords: AI literacy Creativity skills Seizing skills Strategizing capability Startup entrepreneurial capabilities Generation Z students

This study examines the causal relationships among AI literacy, creativity skills, seizing skills, and strategizing capability in shaping startup entrepreneurial readiness among Thai Generation Z management students. A quantitative approach was employed, with data collected from 484 undergraduate students through a validated online questionnaire and analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). The findings indicate that AI literacy signifi­ cantly influences both creativity skills and seizing skills, which subsequently enhance strategizing capability. Strategizing capability emerged as the strongest predictor of students' perceived entrepreneurial readiness, highlighting its central role in integrating technological knowledge, creative thinking, and opportunity-oriented decision-making. These results provide empirical support for the Dynamic Capabilities Framework at the individual level, demonstrating how entrepreneurial readiness develops through sensing, seizing, and strategic integration processes in a student and pre-venture context. The study offers practical implications for higher education by emphasizing the importance of curricula that integrate AI literacy with creative and strategic learning to support students’ preparedness for future entrepreneurial engagement.

1. Introduction

In recent years, entrepreneurship has emerged as a crucial topic of interest among both public and private sectors worldwide, including Thailand, where the government has actively promoted the establishment of startups to drive the new economy based on innovation and technology (Thawesaengskulthai et al., 2024). The rise of new startups not only contributes to employment generation and income distribution but also enhances national competitiveness at the global level (Mehmeti & Musabelli, 2024). However, the success of startup creation and management depends on multiple factors, among which entrepreneurial capability plays a pivotal role in determining the founder's ability to identify opportunities, design strategies, and ensure sustainable growth (Khuong & Van, 2022).

One of the most critical competencies emerging in recent years is AI literacy, which refers to the ability to understand and apply artificial intelligence (AI) technologies to create new business value and enhance operational efficiency (Ng et al., 2021). AI literacy extends beyond coding or developing AI models—it also includes the strategic capability to integrate AI into organizational systems and decision-making processes (Almatrafi et al., 2024). For Gen Z students who are already digitally adept, advancing toward AI literacy presents opportunities to foster business innovation and explore diverse entrepreneurial pathways (Chan & Lee, 2023). Alongside AI literacy, creativity has become another indispensable skill that enables entrepreneurs to develop novel products and services that stand out in the market (Imaobong & Brownson, 2024). In the current technology-driven era, creativity goes beyond artistic expression—it encompasses strategic thinking, problem-solving from new perspectives, and the ability to adapt technologies to different contexts (Daymond & Knight, 2023). For Thai Gen Z management students, who frequently engage in interdisciplinary teamwork, creativity is a key determinant of success in initiating innovative projects or ventures within highly competitive markets (Imjai et al., 2024).

Nevertheless, prior studies often examine AI literacy, creativity, or opportunity-related skills in isolation, without sufficiently explaining how these competencies interact to shape entrepreneurial capability development in a student or pre-venture context. While AI literacy and creativity provide a foundation for innovation, they may not necessarily translate into entrepreneurial readiness without seizing skills. These refer to the ability to detect, evaluate, and capitalize on emerging opportunities while making strategic decisions to allocate resources effectively (Farida & Setiawan, 2022; Kucharska & Karwowska, 2025). In dynamic and uncertain business environments, Gen Z students aspiring to become startup entrepreneurs must continuously develop their opportunity-seizing ability to act decisively and capitalize on transient market windows (Lange et al., 2023). Entrepreneurs who can connect AI literacy with creative thinking are better positioned to design innovative products and services that meet market needs and transform competitive environments (Haleem et al., 2022). Meanwhile, the ability to seize opportunities enables individuals to make informed and timely decisions regarding resource investments, supporting the development of sustainable entrepreneurial pathways (Sieg et al., 2023). However, limited empirical research has simultaneously examined the combined roles of AI literacy, creativity skills, and seizing skills in explaining how entrepreneurial capability readiness is formed among Generation Z management students, particularly in emerging economies such as Thailand.

Accordingly, this research aims to examine the roles of AI literacy, creativity skills, and seizing skills in developing startup entrepreneurial capability readiness among Thai Gen Z management students. By adopting an individual-level perspective, this study seeks to identify the mechanisms through which these competencies jointly support students’ preparedness for future entrepreneurial engagement rather than immediate venture performance. The findings are expected to contribute to both theoretical understanding and practical implications for universities and entrepreneurship development agencies. Furthermore, the study seeks to propose conceptual and pedagogical directions for designing curricula and extracurricular programs that integrate AI, creative thinking, and strategic opportunity management, thereby supporting Thai Gen Z students in developing the competencies required for entrepre­ neurship in the digital era.

2. Literature review

2.1. AI literacy

In the era where technology and innovation play a crucial role in driving organizations at all levels, AI literacy—defined as the knowledge, understanding, and ability to effectively apply artificial intelligence (AI)—has become an essential competency for learners and professionals in the modern workforce (Füller et al., 2022). Within the context of Thai management students, AI literacy goes beyond mere awareness of what AI is; it encompasses understanding the underlying technologies, selecting appropriate AI tools for specific work contexts, and managing the outcomes and risks associated with AI use (Songsiengchai et al., 2023). The ability to use AI effectively enhances learners’ adaptability to complex business environments by supporting innovation, strategic decision-making, and opportunity recognition (Ali et al., 2024).

From a Dynamic Capabilities perspective, AI literacy in this study is conceptualized as an enabling resource rather than a capability in itself. At the individual level, AI literacy functions as a foundational input that supports the development of higher-order process capabilities, such as creativity, opportunity seizing, and strategic thinking, particularly in a student and pre-venture context (Gao et al., 2025). AI literacy consists of four major dimensions. The first is Basic Understanding, which serves as the foundation for applying AI knowledge in practice. It involves understanding the principles of algorithms and machine learning as well as differentiating between various types and applications of AI systems (Bellini et al., 2022). The second dimension is Work Application, reflecting the ability to select and employ appropriate AI tools or platforms suited to specific projects or business tasks (Haleem et al., 2022)—for instance, using AI to analyze customer data to uncover marketing opportunities or optimize team scheduling. A high level of AI literacy not only supports business and analytical learning but also functions as a critical foundation for inno­ vation and the development of future entrepreneurial potential (Yang et al., 2024). Among Generation Z management students, AI literacy has been shown to interact closely with other key competencies, particularly creativity and strategic thinking. Prior studies suggest that individuals with strong AI literacy are more likely to explore novel problem-solving approaches and engage in creative experimentation, as AI tools enable them to reinterpret data, generate alternative solutions, and reconfigure existing knowledge in innovative ways (Singh et al., 2024). For example, students with solid AI foundations can leverage technology as a creative enabler by analyzing customer data to design niche-specific products or by developing innovative, data-driven business models (Stolpe & Hallstr¨om, 2024). These findings indicate that AI literacy serves as an important antecedent to the development of creativity skills in entrepreneurial contexts.

Beyond creativity, AI literacy also enhances students’ capacity to interpret complex business environments and respond to emerging opportunities in a timely and informed manner. In the era of big data and advanced AI systems, individuals with higher levels of AI literacy are better equipped to analyze environmental changes, synthesize large volumes of information, and support decisionmaking processes through data-driven insights (Haleem et al., 2022; Kumar et al., 2024). Moreover, AI literacy facilitates environ­ mental scanning and the evaluation of strategic alternatives, enabling learners to assess risks and opportunities more systematically (Gerassis et al., 2021). Importantly, the ethical dimension of AI literacy further strengthens strategic awareness by encouraging consideration of societal impacts, fairness, and accountability in technology-enabled decision-making (Polat et al., 2025; Uzorka et al., 2024). Taken together, these studies suggest that AI literacy provides a strategic advantage by supporting opportunity recognition, informed decision-making, and adaptive strategic thinking. Accordingly, drawing on the existing literature, this study proposes the following hypotheses:

H1. AI literacy has a positive effect on creativity skills;

H2. AI literacy has a positive effect on seizing skills; and

H3. AI literacy has a positive effect on strategizing capability.

2.2. Creativity skills

In highly competitive business and management environments, creativity is recognized as a critical factor that enables individuals and organizations to gain a competitive advantage, design distinctive products or services, and foster value-driven innovation (Jerab & Mabrouk, 2023). For students preparing to become entrepreneurs or to work in modern organizations, creativity skills go beyond artistic abilities such as drawing or design. They encompass the cognitive capacity to think creatively and apply such thinking in diverse situations, particularly for solving business problems systematically (Gundry et al., 2014).

Creativity skills can be assessed through four measurable indicators. The first is Idea Generation, referring to the ability to brainstorm and produce diverse and novel ideas to address business problems or explore opportunities (Henningsen & Henningsen, 2013). This capability allows organizations to initiate projects or innovations with a higher likelihood of future success. The second is Flexible Thinking, which reflects the ability to adapt perspectives or modify problem-solving approaches without being constrained by pre-existing ideas. Flexibility enables learners or entrepreneurs to manage unexpected situations and discover more effective ways of working (Barak & Levenberg, 2016). The third indicator is Cross-disciplinary Application, defined as the ability to integrate knowl­ edge, skills, and experiences from multiple disciplines to solve problems or generate innovative ideas (Borda et al., 2022). The capacity to combine knowledge from fields such as technology, marketing, and finance enhances the potential for developing products or processes that offer genuine differentiation. Finally, Creative Expression involves effectively communicating and presenting creative ideas to others in a way that highlights their value and feasibility (Tabuena, 2022). This skill is particularly crucial when presenting business plans or project proposals to stakeholders such as investors, clients, and team members to gain understanding, support, and collaboration.

Creativity skills play a vital role in contemporary social and economic environments characterized by rapid change and uncer­ tainty. Prior studies emphasize that creativity enables individuals to remain competitive by fostering flexibility, openness to diverse perspectives, and continuous innovation at both personal and organizational levels (Huang et al., 2022; Elbaz et al., 2025). In business contexts, flexible thinking and cross-disciplinary application allow students and entrepreneurs to interpret opportunities and risks from multiple angles, thereby supporting more informed and adaptive decision-making (Fragapane et al., 2022). In addition, creative expression enhances strategic communication by enabling individuals to articulate ideas, strategies, and visions more persuasively to team members and external stakeholders (Atkoˇci¯unien˙e et al., 2024).

Within startup settings, creativity is increasingly recognized as a critical driver of entrepreneurial development, extending beyond technical knowledge or access to capital. Entrepreneurs with strong creativity skills are more inclined to pursue novel solutions, recombine existing resources in innovative ways, and generate differentiated value propositions, particularly during the early stages of venture formation (Skala & Skala, 2019; Somwethee et al., 2023). 2.3. Seizing skills

In today's volatile and highly competitive business environment, seizing skills—the ability to identify and capitalize on opportu­ nities—have become a critical determinant of organizational adaptability and sustainable profitability (Garavan et al., 2016). This concept is grounded in the Dynamic Capabilities Framework, which explains that to sustain long-term competitiveness, organizations and their leaders must be able to sense, adapt to, and seize new opportunities before their competitors (Teece, 2016). In the context of Thai management students, these skills represent a central component of entrepreneurial development, enabling individuals to recognize business opportunities, make informed and timely decisions, and manage resources and risks effectively (Promma et al., 2023). At the individual level, seizing skills are conceptualized as process-based capabilities rather than realized entrepreneurial out­ comes. In the context of students and pre-venture actors, these skills reflect cognitive and behavioral processes related to opportunity evaluation, decision readiness, and resource prioritization, which contribute to capability development rather than immediate busi­ ness performance (Perez et al., 2026). Seizing skills can be captured through four key dimensions. The first is Opportunity Recognition, which refers to the ability to identify and monitor new business opportunities or project ideas accurately. Recognizing unmet market needs or gaps before competitors is a critical foundation for competitive advantage (Autio et al., 2013). The second dimension is Timely Decision-making, which entails making swift yet well-informed decisions based on sufficient analysis and data (Abubakar et al., 2019). This skill is essential in environments where opportunities may appear and disappear rapidly, allowing those who act promptly to capture limited market resources ahead of others. The third dimension, Resource Allocation, concerns the effective management and distribution of financial, human, and time resources to maximize value and performance (Symeonidou et al., 2022). Entrepreneurs who can strategically prioritize limited resources are better positioned to exploit opportunities efficiently. The fourth dimension is Risk Management, which involves the ability to anticipate, evaluate, and mitigate risks associated with new ventures or opportunities (George, 2020). While opportunity pursuit drives innovation and growth, the absence of sound risk management can lead to financial losses or business failure.

Seizing skills play a pivotal role in shaping both business survival and growth, particularly in environments characterized by uncertainty and rapid change. Prior studies highlight that individuals with strong seizing skills are better able to assess competitive conditions, recognize time-sensitive opportunities, and act strategically at critical moments (Tatham et al., 2017). Empirical evidence suggests that opportunity recognition, timely decision-making, effective resource allocation, and proactive risk management jointly enable entrepreneurs to respond decisively to emerging market conditions and to transform potential opportunities into actionable initiatives (Deep, 2023). These competencies collectively support the development of higher-order strategic processes, as they provide the informational and behavioral inputs necessary for formulating coherent strategies.

In the context of emerging entrepreneurs, seizing skills are also closely associated with early-stage venture outcomes. The ability to identify optimal timing for product or service launches, mobilize key human and financial resources, and manage uncertainties inherent in business experimentation has been shown to enhance investor confidence and reduce the likelihood of early venture failure (Maine et al., 2015; Skala & Skala, 2019). However, while seizing skills facilitate opportunity exploitation and resource mobilization, prior literature suggests that their contribution to entrepreneurial outcomes may operate both directly and indirectly—through their influence on strategizing capability, which enables entrepreneurs to integrate opportunities into structured plans and long-term di­ rections. Taken together, existing studies indicate that seizing skills function not only as a driver of entrepreneurial action but also as a critical mechanism linking opportunity recognition to strategic capability and venture-related outcomes. Accordingly, based on this body of literature, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H6. Seizing skills positively influence strategizing capability; and

H7. Seizing skills positively influence startup entrepreneurial capabilities.

2.4. Strategizing capability

In the field of business management research, strategizing capability refers to an individual's or organization's capacity to formulate clear directions and appropriate courses of action that enable the attainment of long-term goals and the creation of a sustainable competitive advantage (Zapata, 2020). For Generation Z students who are preparing to enter the business world or laying the foun­ dation for future startup ventures, strategizing capability is particularly crucial. This importance stems from the fact that today's digital business environment is characterized by complexity, rapid change, and intense competition (Skala & Skala, 2019).

Strategic planning and implementation that are systematic and well-coordinated are widely recognized as critical determinants of startup entrepreneurial outcomes. Prior studies suggest that individuals who possess strong strategizing capability are better equipped to define clear business directions, identify niche market opportunities, and formulate unique value propositions that differentiate their ventures from competitors (Atanasova, 2021). In early-stage startup contexts, such capabilities enable entrepreneurs to translate abstract ideas and emerging opportunities into structured plans, thereby reducing uncertainty and enhancing the feasibility of venture development. Moreover, strategizing capability has been identified as a central driver of startup success by supporting goal alignment, resource prioritization, and adaptive decision-making in dynamic market environments (Crnogaj & Rus, 2023). Rather than operating in isolation, strategizing capability integrates insights derived from creativity and opportunity-seizing processes into coherent strategic actions that guide venture growth and sustainability. Accordingly, the literature indicates that strategizing capability functions as a key mechanism through which entrepreneurial competencies are transformed into tangible startup outcomes. 2.5. Startup entrepreneurial capabilities

In the modern economy driven by innovation and technology, Startup Entrepreneurial Capabilities (SEC) represent a crucial construct that reflects individuals’ potential and perceived preparedness to initiate, establish, and sustainably expand new ventures (Serio et al., 2020). This concept encompasses multiple facets of entrepreneurial competence, including readiness for venture creation, business innovation ability, scalability orientation, and partnership formation—all of which are integral to the future viability and competitiveness of startups (Lange et al., 2023). For Thai Generation Z students or aspiring young entrepreneurs, possessing strong startup entrepreneurial capabilities supports their readiness to transform creative ideas and business opportunities into ventures that may generate both economic and social value (Hossain et al., 2023). The construct of startup entrepreneurial capabilities consists of four key indicators. First, Venture Creation Readiness refers to the perceived level of preparedness in terms of skills, mindset, and resources necessary for launching a new business. It involves the ability to develop business plans, conduct feasibility analyses, and assess potential risks during the early stages of venture establishment (Wenninger, 2019). Among Thai Generation Z management students, those who exhibit readiness in this dimension typically demonstrate a growth mindset coupled with an understanding of basic legal and procedural requirements for business initiation.

Second, Business Innovation Ability denotes the perceived capacity to generate, develop, and apply new ideas or technologies to create innovative products, services, or processes that distinguish a venture from competitors (Zastempowski, 2022). In a student and pre-venture context, innovation capability reflects creative and solution-oriented thinking that is widely recognized as a critical foundation for future entrepreneurial value creation (Kongrode et al., 2023).

Third, Growth Expansion Focus refers to the orientation and intention toward future business scaling, once the initial phase has achieved a certain level of success (Coviello et al., 2024). The concept of growth orientation is a core characteristic that differentiates startup-oriented thinking from traditional small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), as scalability enables ventures to capture larger markets and strengthen their position in the innovation ecosystem. Finally, Partnership Formation highlights the perceived capability and readiness to build and maintain strategic networks and collaborations with individuals or organizations that share mutual ben­ efits—such as investors, business partners, educational institutions, or governmental agencies (McQuaid, 2000). For startups, success often accelerates through partnerships that complement internal strengths, mitigate weaknesses, or provide access to funding, tech­ nology, and broader markets. For aspiring entrepreneurs, evaluating competencies in these areas and seeking collaboration with experienced partners is considered an important preparatory step toward sustainable business development (Brattstr¨om, 2019). By cultivating these four dimensions, aspiring entrepreneurs and students are better prepared to pursue long-term venture development and adapt effectively to changing market trends and technological disruptions.

3.1. Research design

This study employed a quantitative research design to examine and analyze the factors influencing the Startup Entrepreneurial Capabilities (SEC) of Thai Generation Z management students who are currently enrolled in undergraduate and postgraduate programs at higher education institutions across the country. This population represents a cohort that has grown up in the digital era, is highly familiar with technology from an early age, and possesses a cross-disciplinary learning orientation that supports the development of innovation and creativity essential for new-generation entrepreneurship. The research was designed to investigate the in­ terrelationships among five principal constructs: AI Literacy (AIL), Creativity Skills (CRS), Seizing Skills (SES), Strategizing Capability (STC), and Startup Entrepreneurial Capabilities (SEC). Data were collected using an online survey questionnaire, which served as the main research instrument. The questionnaire was distributed via email, social media platforms, and academic networks of students enrolled in business administration and related disciplines. These channels were selected to align with the digital communication behavior of Generation Z students and to maximize the representativeness and accessibility of the sample.

Prior to completing the questionnaire, respondents were provided with a brief introduction to the core constructs—AI Literacy, Creativity Skills, Seizing Skills, Strategizing Capability, and Startup Entrepreneurial Capabilities—to ensure conceptual clarity and minimize response bias arising from differing interpretations. A convenience sampling technique was adopted due to the absence of an exact population frame of Thai Generation Z management students. In addition, convenience sampling has been widely employed in entrepreneurship and education research involving Generation Z students, particularly when the research objective focuses on theory testing and model estimation rather than population generalization (Hair et al., 2019). In management and business-related programs, female students are typically overrepresented, which may result in a higher proportion of female respondents in survey-based research. This characteristic is therefore acknowledged as a contextual feature of the sample rather than a methodological anomaly (Welsh et al., 2017). The sample size was determined using Cochran's formula for an infinite population, yielding a minimum recommended sample size of 384 participants. To enhance the reliability of the data and reduce potential non-response bias, the researcher collected 484 complete responses, which were deemed sufficient for conducting advanced statistical analyses such as Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) using SmartPLS software. Prior to participation, all respondents were informed about the research objectives, the voluntary nature of their participation, and the confidentiality of their responses. Informed consent was obtained electronically before re­ spondents proceeded with the questionnaire, and no personally identifiable information was collected. This research design ensured the collection of comprehensive and diverse quantitative data, effectively capturing the perceptions and experiences of Thai Gener­ ation Z management students. It also aligns with the overall research objective—to empirically analyze the roles of AI literacy and entrepreneurial-related skills in shaping the startup entrepreneurial capabilities of Thailand's emerging young entrepreneurs.

The primary instrument used for data collection in this study was a structured questionnaire, developed based on an extensive review of relevant literature pertaining to the five main constructs: AI Literacy (AIL), Creativity Skills (CRS), Seizing Skills (SES), Strategizing Capability (STC), and Startup Entrepreneurial Capabilities (SEC). The questionnaire was specifically designed to reflect the learning characteristics of Thai Generation Z management students, who typically engage in blended learning environments that integrate technology and innovative thinking. The development process followed a systematic approach. Initially, questionnaire items were designed to comprehensively cover all five core constructs according to the conceptual framework of the study. The draft version of the questionnaire was then reviewed by three experts specializing in management, technology, and entrepreneurship development to assess content validity and ensure the linguistic appropriateness of the items within the Thai Gen Z educational context. The questionnaire was administered primarily in Thai to ensure clarity and comprehension among respondents. All measurement items were adapted from established English-language sources and translated into Thai using a back-translation procedure to ensure se­ mantic equivalence and conceptual consistency across languages. Their feedback was incorporated to refine the clarity, accuracy, and comprehensiveness of the instrument in alignment with the research objectives. The final questionnaire comprised two main sections.

3.3. Data analysis methods

Data analysis in this study began with descriptive statistics to summarize the demographic characteristics of the respondents and to present the mean levels of indicators across the five latent variables: AI Literacy, Creativity Skills, Seizing Skills, Strategizing Capa­ bility, and Startup Entrepreneurial Capabilities. The preliminary statistical analysis employed mean (M) and standard deviation (SD) values to interpret data tendencies. For ease of qualitative interpretation, the mean scores were classified into five levels: very low (0.00–1.00), low (1.01–2.00), moderate (2.01–3.00), high (3.01–4.00), and very high (4.01–5.00). Subsequently, causal relationships among the variables were examined using the Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) technique with the SmartPLS software package. This approach was chosen for its suitability in analyzing complex structural models that include multiple latent and observed variables. The measurement model was evaluated in terms of reliability and validity following established sta­ tistical criteria: indicator loadings (≥0.70), composite reliability (CR ≥0.70), convergent validity (AVE ≥0.50), and discriminant validity based on the Fornell–Larcker criterion. For the structural model, the analysis assessed collinearity issues through the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF), coefficient of determination (R2), predictive relevance (Q2), and predictive performance using the PLSpredict analysis, following the methodological guidelines of Hair et al. (2019).

Finally, path analysis was conducted to test the research hypotheses by examining the path coefficients between the independent variables—AI Literacy, Creativity Skills, Seizing Skills, and Strategizing Capability—and the dependent variable, Startup Entrepre­ neurial Capabilities. The statistical significance (p-values) of each path was used to confirm the hypothesized relationships among constructs. 4. Results

5. Discussion

The findings of this study provide empirical support for the argument that AI literacy functions as a foundational developmental resource that enhances higher-order entrepreneurial competencies among Thai Gen Z management students. Specifically, the strong relationships between AI Literacy, Creativity Skills, and Seizing Skills suggest that students who possess a deeper understanding of AI technologies are better prepared to employ such knowledge in exploratory and opportunity-oriented ways. From a theoretical perspective, this finding aligns with the Dynamic Capabilities Framework by positioning AI literacy as an enabling input that strengthens sensing-related processes, rather than as a direct driver of strategic capability. This interpretation is consistent with prior studies emphasizing that AI literacy extends beyond technical familiarity to include the ability to interpret outputs, reflect on im­ plications, and creatively leverage insights for innovation (Bellini et al., 2022; Füller et al., 2022; Haleem et al., 2022).

In addition, the significant effects of Creativity Skills and Seizing Skills on Strategizing Capability underscore the importance of intermediate capability-building mechanisms in entrepreneurial development. The results indicate that flexible thinking, idea gen­ eration, and opportunity recognition do not automatically translate into entrepreneurial outcomes unless they are organized and directed through strategic processes. Within the Dynamic Capabilities Framework, this pattern reflects the transition from sensing and seizing activities toward transforming capabilities, operationalized in this study as strategizing capability at the individual level. This finding corroborates earlier work suggesting that creativity and opportunity recognition must be supported by goal setting, option development, and strategic communication to become actionable (Cengiz, 2020; Pervaiz et al., 2021; Tong & Reuer, 2007). Accordingly, strategizing capability emerges as a critical mediating mechanism that converts exploratory skills into readiness-oriented entrepreneurial competence.

With regard to unsupported hypotheses, the non-significant direct relationship between AI Literacy and Strategizing Capability suggests that technological knowledge alone may be insufficient for strategic reasoning without complementary experiential and cognitive processes. This finding refines existing literature by indicating that, in a student context, AI literacy primarily strengthens upstream capabilities (creativity and seizing) rather than directly shaping strategic formulation. Strategic capability development appears to require experiential learning, contextual judgment, and iterative reflection—elements that are often limited among students who have not yet engaged in real-world entrepreneurial decision-making. This interpretation aligns with prior research emphasizing the role of practice-based learning in translating digital knowledge into strategic competence (Atanasova, 2021).

Similarly, the absence of a significant direct effect of Seizing Skills on Startup Entrepreneurial Capabilities can be explained by the readiness-based nature of the dependent construct and the characteristics of the sample. While opportunity recognition and timely decision-making are essential entrepreneurial traits, their impact may remain latent at the pre-venture stage unless integrated with strategic planning and execution capabilities. For university students, seizing skills may reflect perceived potential rather than realized entrepreneurial performance, reinforcing the view that entrepreneurial capability development is a staged and cumulative process (Crnogaj & Rus, 2023). Rather than contradicting prior findings, this result highlights the importance of sequencing capability development, particularly in educational contexts.

Overall, this study advances theoretical understanding by demonstrating how AI literacy, creativity, and seizing skills interact within a readiness-oriented entrepreneurial capability framework among Thai Gen Z management students. By adapting the Dynamic Capabilities Framework to the individual level, the findings illustrate how sensing (AI literacy and creativity), seizing (opportunityoriented skills), and transforming (strategizing capability) collectively shape students’ preparedness for future entrepreneurial engagement. This integrated perspective emphasizes that entrepreneurial capability among students should be viewed not as a static outcome but as a developmental process that unfolds through learning, reflection, and strategic integration in the digital era.

5.1. The dynamics of new-generation entrepreneurs in the age of artificial intelligence

The findings of this study reveal the dynamic learning behaviors and entrepreneurial preparedness of the new generation in an era where Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become deeply integrated into everyday life and the modern economic system. This is particularly evident among Generation Z students, who have grown up surrounded by digital media and blended learning environments. The finding that AI literacy significantly influences both creativity skills and seizing skills reflects a structural transformation in 21st-cen­ tury learning—where technological knowledge increasingly functions as a foundational enabler of creative thinking and opportunityoriented learning, rather than merely as a supporting tool. In the contemporary educational and pre-venture context, AI is no longer confined to computer laboratories but is increasingly incorporated across multiple dimensions of management learning, including consumer data analysis, market trend interpretation, idea prototyping, and project coordination. 5.2. Limitations of the study

This study is subject to several limitations that should be acknowledged when interpreting the findings. First, the research relied on a cross-sectional quantitative survey administered to Thai Generation Z management students. While this approach is appropriate for examining structural relationships among key constructs, it may not fully capture the dynamic and experiential processes through which entrepreneurial capabilities are developed over time. In particular, most respondents were still in formal education and had limited direct exposure to real-world startup operations. As a result, the measurement of startup entrepreneurial capabilities primarily reflects students’ perceived readiness and developmental potential rather than realized entrepreneurial performance.

Second, the use of convenience sampling, although suitable given the absence of a comprehensive population frame, may limit the generalizability of the findings. The sample composition also reflects the contextual characteristics of management and business programs in Thailand, where female students are typically overrepresented. This demographic imbalance may influence perceptions of entrepreneurial readiness and technology-related competencies. Moreover, the study is situated within a specific cultural and insti­ tutional context shaped by Thailand's education system, digital infrastructure, and policy environment. These contextual factors may affect how AI literacy and entrepreneurial-related skills are developed and expressed, thereby constraining the direct applicability of the findings to other national or cultural settings. Finally, data were collected through self-reported online questionnaires, which may introduce common method bias or social desirability effects. Respondents who are more comfortable with digital technologies may have been more inclined to participate, potentially inflating reported levels of AI literacy and related competencies. Although pro­ cedural remedies were applied to mitigate these issues, such limitations remain inherent in survey-based research designs.

5.3. Directions for future research

Building on the limitations of this study, several directions for future research are recommended. First, future studies should extend the investigation beyond student samples to include active startup founders, early-stage entrepreneurs, or participants in startup incubation and acceleration programs. Such samples would allow researchers to compare perceived entrepreneurial readiness with actual entrepreneurial behavior and performance, thereby strengthening the external validity of the proposed framework. Second, longitudinal research designs are strongly encouraged. Tracking the same individuals over time would provide deeper insights into how AI literacy, creativity, seizing skills, and strategizing capability evolve and interact throughout different stages of entrepreneurial development. Longitudinal approaches would also enable a clearer assessment of causal relationships and the transformation of readiness-based capabilities into realized entrepreneurial outcomes. Third, future research should consider employing mixed-method designs that integrate quantitative modeling with qualitative approaches such as in-depth interviews, case studies, or reflective learning journals. These methods would help uncover the underlying cognitive, motivational, and contextual mechanisms through which AI literacy is translated into strategic and entrepreneurial action. Finally, comparative studies across countries, educational systems, or cultural contexts would be particularly valuable. Such research could examine how institutional support, cultural norms, and digital ecosystems shape the development of AI-related competencies and entrepreneurial capabilities. 6. Conclusion

This study aimed to examine the relationships among AI Literacy, Creativity Skills, Seizing Skills, and Strategizing Capability, and their collective influence on the Startup Entrepreneurial Capabilities of Thai Generation Z management students. The findings revealed that AI literacy serves as a foundational factor that directly enhances both creativity and opportunity-seizing skills. These two skill sets, in turn, function as critical mechanisms that strengthen strategic capability, ultimately contributing to the development of startup entrepreneurial capabilities. Among all constructs, Strategizing Capability emerged as the most influential mediating variable within the structural model. The results affirm that the development of new-generation entrepreneurs cannot be achieved solely through teaching technology or creativity in isolation; rather, it requires an integrated approach that combines technical knowledge, cognitive competencies, and systematic strategic thinking. From a theoretical perspective, this study advances the understanding of AI literacy within the framework of Dynamic Capabilities Theory, which explains entrepreneurial capability development through the mecha­ nisms of sensing and seizing opportunities, followed by strategizing and transforming. Practically, the findings provide valuable implications for educational institutions and entrepreneurship development agencies in designing curricula or programs that foster integrative learning between technology and strategic thinking. Examples include startup project-based learning, work-integrated learning programs, and AI-driven business simulation labs. These approaches can help Thai Generation Z students bridge their AI knowledge with creative and strategic decision-making skills, thereby enhancing their readiness to become capable startup entre­ preneurs who are competitive in the digital marketplace and capable of driving Thailand's innovation-driven economy toward a sustainable future.
