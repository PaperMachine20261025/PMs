Higher education institutions are vital in shaping responsible citizens with the skills to address global challenges. This research paper explores the development of social intelligence competency among higher education students attending Tecnologico de Monterrey (TEC), Mexico. TEC, a leading institution focusing on 21st-century skills, integrates transversal competencies, including social intelligence, into its educational model. Our study investigated factors inﬂuencing the acquisition of soft skills, the role of academic institutions in promoting social intelligence, and the beneﬁts for students. We proposed a set of hypotheses postulating collaborative learning, interactivity, social presence, engagement, and development of social intelligence competency as interrelated constructs. To investigate these relationships, we conducted a survey and designed speciﬁc course activities to gauge the eﬀectiveness of social intelligence interventions. We administered the Google Forms survey to 62 students enrolled in an industrial design program course. The outcomes were scanned and examined using IBM SPSS version 26 software. Our hypotheses posited signiﬁcant associations between independent variables (collaborative learning, interactivity, social presence, engagement) and the dependent variable (students’ social intelligence development). This study contributes to understanding the importance of social intelligence in higher education and oﬀers insights for educators and institutions on how to prioritize its development.

2. Relevant Literature and Hypotheses Development

2.1 Collaborative Learning

Collaborative learning must be a fundamental component in developing collective awareness within a group. This method harnesses the range of identities within each working group. The unique motivations and personalities of individual students within the group dynamics emerging from their interactions shape a distinct collaborative learning approach that is challenging to regulate and deﬁne [20]. It generates fresh knowledge and ideas beyond what each group member possesses individually [21]. Extensive research has thoroughly documented the positive outcomes and merits of eﬀective collaborative learning. These include cultivating self-esteem, reducing anxiety, encouraging diversity, augmenting critical thinking abilities, and developing other advanced skills [22]. Collaborative learning functions as a natural setting for acquiring and enhancing social skills. This is primarily attributed to the fundamental role of social interaction in developing social intelligence. Therefore, promoting and facilitating social interaction among individuals signiﬁcantly improves their socio-cognitive comprehension [23]. Encouraging social interaction among students enhances their socio-cognitive understanding, enabling the development of higher-quality social interactions. Collaborative learning fosters a sense of community and support

among students, essential when physical isolation becomes necessary. Through online platforms, collaborative learning can be maintained via group projects, virtual study groups, and peer-to-peer interactions. This will not only help in the continuation of academic activities but also ensure that students remain connected and engaged [24]. Online collaborative learning fosters engagement between students and teachers while building a social presence [25]. These interactions can help alleviate student isolation without direct interpersonal interaction in distance learning, which is especially crucial during challenging periods like the COVID-19 pandemic [26, 27].

2.2 Interactivity

The Oxford Online Dictionary [28] deﬁnes interactivity as a process where people work together and inﬂuence each other. Studies have shown that interactive learning enhances students’ engagement, boosts their motivation, increases their attentiveness, facilitates timely and comprehensive feedback, improves their comprehension, enhances problem-solving skills, and promotes critical thinking, leading to improved participation and performance [29]. For eﬀective interactive learning, instructors must transition from traditional lecturing to facilitating [30]. The learning process in the educational environment engages diverse forms of communication, cultivating social skills for eﬀective interactions and helping students understand the signiﬁcance of social engagement. Social competency employs self-assuredness, adeptness in social interactions, creativity, achieving social objectives, and self-motivation. Learning by teaching can trigger underlying cognitive processes, including selecting pertinent information, organizing it into coherent structures, and integrating it with existing knowledge. These processes can lead to enduring learning outcomes [31]. Beyond cognitive advantages, learning by teaching may also positively impact individuals’ self-monitoring abilities. Interactive learning activities, such as discussions, simulations, and problem-solving sessions, help students stay mentally active and reduce the feelings of isolation and stress accompanying emergencies [18]. Tools like discussion forums, interactive webinars, and real-time quizzes enable students to interact with instructors and peers, creating a dynamic and engaging learning environment even when they are physically apart. Online learning systems help reduce resistance to changing environments like the COVID-19 pandemic but must

incorporate interactivity. Online learning enhances students’ interactivity and improves their readiness for online study, so students prioritize information quality in building a high-quality education [32].

2.3 Social Presence

Social presence has been deﬁned as the capacity to convey social-communicative cues, such as nonverbal language. Some theories of education, including social learning and constructivism, propose that social processes have a highly relevant role in learning and teaching [33]. Poth (2018) stated that ‘‘determining how to develop an individual’s ‘social presence’ within the learning environment is key to promoting a more engaging and supportive educational experience, in which students become more motivated and can attain more success’’ [34]. As per Whiteside (2014), social presence pertains to establishing a connection between educators and students, inﬂuencing students’ active engagement in their own and their peers’ meaning-making processes [35]. Moreover, learning is characterized by exchanges in social activities that produce understanding from abstract information [36]. Thus, creating social experiences for students is vital in fostering an eﬃcient learning environment. During a pandemic like COVID-19, social presence plays a crucial role in online learning, as many students need to feel connected to others to share ideas, exchange perspectives, and collaborate [37]. Maintaining a strong social presence in online and remote learning scenarios helps mitigate emergencies’ emotional and psychological impacts. It reassures students that they are part of a supportive community. Video conferencing tools, social media, and virtual oﬃce hours enhance social presence, ensuring students feel seen and heard, thus fostering a sense of belonging and community [38].

students’ development of social intelligence competency.

2.4 Engagement

Learning engagement is a multifaceted concept encompassing cognitive, behavioral, and emotional dimensions. Gunuc and A. Kuzu [39] oﬀered an additional perspective on student engagement, deﬁning it as ‘‘the depth and breadth of students’ psychological, cognitive, emotional, and behavioral responses to the learning process, both in and out of the classroom, aimed at achieving successful learning outcomes (p. 588).’’ Behavioral engagement con-

centrates on factors such as adherence to rules, classroom conduct, regular attendance, diligent completion of assignments, and interest in extracurricular activities [40]. Emotional engagement pertains to students’ feelings and responses during learning [41]. This includes enjoyment, enthusiasm, interest, belonging, and eagerness to participate in class. Cognitive engagement concerns students’ utilization of self-regulated learning, deep learning strategies, and their comprehension of complex concepts. Aﬀective engagement encompasses joy, enthusiasm, connection, belonging, and positive interactions with others and the school environment. Some authors have observed that students displaying much enthusiasm and optimism towards their learning tend to exhibit higher levels of academic engagement, as noted by [42]. Simultaneously, these optimistic attitudes, often linked to positive psychological capital, directly contribute to enhanced academic performance [43]. Consequently, engagement can be viewed as a psychological state reﬂecting students’ well-being and intrinsic motivation to contribute positively to their academic tasks [44]. This connection between positive attitudes, engagement, and academic performance underscores the role of social intelligence in fostering a conducive learning environment. High levels of engagement are critical during emergencies to keep students motivated and focused on their learning objectives despite the uncertainties [45]. Engaging content, gamiﬁed learning experiences, and interactive assignments keep students involved in their studies, maintaining their academic progress and mental well-being. Student engagement in technology-mediated learning is a signiﬁcant challenge that can impact the eﬀectiveness of online education [46, 47]. During the COVID-19 pandemic, online student engagement was linked to critical outcomes like higher education students’ satisfaction, perceived learning, and persistence [48, 49].

2.5 Development of Social Intelligence Competency

Social intelligence, as commonly deﬁned by most researchers, refers to the ability to eﬀectively comprehend and evaluate one’s actions and the actions of others. On the other hand, life skills empower individuals to adapt to their environment and cultivate self-management. Core competencies, encompassing intellectual, social, personal, and emotional skills, are crucial for developing profound and lifelong learning [50]. Previous research

has shown that students with socio-emotional competencies tend to maintain socio-emotional balance, leading to improved academic performance and a positive outlook on their achievements [51]. Furthermore, social-emotional competence has been demonstrated to signiﬁcantly inﬂuence social support. It has been established that individuals with higher levels of social-emotional competence are more adept at recognizing and utilizing social support [52, 53]. Innovative education represents a novel approach to learning and a fresh mindset. This approach emphasizes not the mere dissemination of knowledge, which is perpetually evolving and becoming outdated, but rather the acquisition of fundamental competencies that empower individuals to acquire knowledge independently. Competency-based education emphasizes eﬀective communication and collaboration, integral to social intelligence. Students are encouraged to collaborate, share ideas, and engage in constructive dialogue, honing their interpersonal skills [54]. There has been a growing demand for qualiﬁed graduates in civil engineering who can tackle complex problems by utilizing personal and professional qualities such as social intelligence and eﬀective self-presentation in their design processes [55]. Social intelligence encompasses navigating social interactions eﬀectively, understanding and managing emotions, and building relationships, all of which are invaluable during emergencies [56]. In an era of continuous change and unstable environments, engineers with vital social intelligence are better prepared to [57]:

Engineers face challenges due to the need for more practice of communication skills and the limited education of social intelligence in their programs [58]. Despite knowing these social skills are vital for their careers, engineering graduates identiﬁed essential skills needed in their ﬁrst jobs, including communication, teamwork, problem-solving, and

Industrial engineers need both technical and social training and time to develop these skills [59] Improving communication, empathy, and social adaptation can help them manage cooperative relationships better [57]. Problem-solving is at the heart of engineering, and social intelligence enhances this by allowing students to approach problems from multiple perspectives and develop innovative solutions. Traditionally seen as a technical ﬁeld, engineering increasingly recognizes the importance of social skills for professional success and eﬀective teamwork [60]..

3. Methods

3.1 Data Collection and Procedure

In August 2023, our research project commenced with students enrolled in the third semester of the Industrial Design Bachelor’s Program at Tecnologico de Monterrey. We created an online survey using Google Forms to gather data from this group. Per university research guidelines, all participants had to provide their consent. We drafted consent statements emphasizing the conﬁdentiality, privacy, and security of the participants’ responses. All participating students willingly volunteered for the survey; they remained anonymous throughout the process.

In October 2023, we distributed the Google Forms online survey to students currently enrolled in a design course in the Industrial Design program oﬀered by the School of Architecture, Arts, and Design at TEC. This course introduced students to product and visual design and various design processes and materials.

To measure levels of social intelligence, we experimented with the three classrooms selected, altering the work method for one of the groups. In this experiment, the control group performed all tasks as a team, practicing collaborative learning, social presence, interactivity, and engagement, while the other two groups completed the activities individually. By the end of the experiment, we observed diﬀerences in the time it took students to complete tasks, their learning abilities, and their social behaviors during the activities. Additionally, we noted the development of soft skills, such as asking peers or instructors for help when needed.

After experimenting, we administered the designed survey, and after reviewing the results, the control group showed more favorable responses regarding the independent variable. Developing social intelligence through these educational practices equips students with crucial skills for challenging tasks. Social intelligence encompasses navigating social interactions eﬀectively, understanding and managing emotions, and building relationships.

3.1.1 Pilot Survey

To ensure the reliability of each question design for the variables and to receive feedback on the survey’s eﬀectiveness, we conducted an initial data collection pilot study involving eight scholarship students. We formally requested these students to complete the online survey voluntarily and provided them with the necessary links. Note that the data from the initial pilot survey was not included in the ﬁnal analysis of our study. We gathered input from students through the pilot survey to evaluate the survey’s structure and wording of questions. Subsequently, we performed a reliability test using IBM SPSS version 26 software to assess each question for every variable. Questions that received a score exceeding (Cronbach Alpha,  > 0.6) were considered appropriate for inclusion in the survey.

3.1.2 Sampling Approach

Before commencing our research, we developed a series of activities intended for one of the three groups enrolled in the course. These activities aimed to incorporate collaborative elements into the class, enhancing soft skills that contribute to developing social intelligence competency. The other two groups experienced the original course design and activities.

3.2 Instruments

The online survey was meticulously crafted to explore various research topics, including interactivity, social presence, engagement, and the impact of collaborative learning on students’ academic performance. We adapted questionnaires from notable sources for our dependent and independent variables to create this survey.

3.2.1 Independent Variables

For engagement and interactivity, we designed three items for each variable, drawing questions from the work of Blasco-Arcas et al. [61], using a ﬁve-point Likert scale, with ‘‘1’’ indicating ‘‘Completely disagree’’ and ‘‘5’’ indicating ‘‘Completely agree.’’

For the collaborative learning variable, we designed three items, adapting questionnaires from So and Brush [62] and Blasco-Arcas et al. [61], using a ﬁve-point Likert scale, with ‘‘1’’ indicating ‘‘Completely disagree’’ and ‘‘5’’ indicating ‘‘Completely agree.’’

To assess social presence, we designed four items, adapting survey questionnaires from Law et al. [63], using a ﬁve-point Likert scale, with ‘‘1’’ indicating ‘‘Strongly disagree’’ and ‘‘5’’ indicating ‘‘Strongly agree.’’

3.2.2 Dependent Variables

We created three items for the assessment of social intelligence competency. We adapted survey questionnaires from de Vos et al. [64], using a ﬁve-point Likert scale, with ‘‘1’’ indicating ‘‘Completely disagree’’ and ‘‘5’’ indicating ‘‘Completely agree.’’

3.2.3 Survey Structure

In addition to core survey questions, we examined demographic variables like gender, scholarship status, enrollment type (full-time or part-time), and instructor identity. The online survey was structured into six sections for clarity and ease of navigation. The ﬁrst section handled conﬁdentiality and gathered control variable data, including student demographics. The second section focused on collaborative learning, the third on interactivity, the fourth on social presence, the ﬁfth on engagement, and the sixth on social intelligence.

3.3 Data Scanning and Analysis

Our formal analysis commenced after conducting the online survey, which encompassed various data management tasks. These included downloading the dataset ﬁle from Google Forms into Excel, data cleaning, assigning unique codes to each response, and labeling variables appropriately. Subsequently, the curated dataset was imported from Microsoft Excel into the IBM SPSS version 26 software application for more extensive statistical analysis.

The initial analytical step involved generating descriptive statistics to gain insights into the respondents’ characteristics, presented in frequency and percentage distributions. In the following step, we conducted an Alpha Cronbach analysis to validate the reliability of each question within every variable. During the third step, we utilized Pearson correlation to validate the relationships among the variables. For the last step, we applied linear regression analysis to statistically verify the connection between the set of dependent and independent variables [41].

5. Discussion, Contribution of the Study and Limitations

5.1 Discussion

This study aimed to prove how collaborative learning, engagement, interactivity, and social presence could impact the development of social intelligence competency in higher education students through extensive research conducted by reliable authors.

The initial ﬁndings of our study revealed that collaborative learning signiﬁcantly enhanced the development of social intelligence competency. Our research aligns with previous investigations that investigated the process of collaborative learning with creative problem-solving and discovered that collaborative learning contributes signiﬁcantly to proactive group learning and improves university students’ academic performance. They explored the inﬂuence of social presence, interactions (among students and with teachers), and emotional engagement on active, collaborative learning on social web platforms, revealing the importance of a positive disposition toward collaboration and the connection between students’ social abilities and their attitudes in collaborative problem-solving [66–69].

The second set of ﬁndings from our research highlights the positive impact of engagement on developing social intelligence competency. Our study is consistent with prior research, including investigations of group awareness tools and their eﬀects on students’ engagement, achievements, and perspectives in online collaborative writing. 5.2 Theoretical Contribution and Practical Implications

The study’s contributions lie in its empirical validation of the interconnectedness between critical factors (collaborative learning, interactivity, social presence, engagement) and the development of social intelligence among higher education students. By presenting concrete evidence through statistical analysis and ﬁndings aligned with prior research, this study enriches the discourse on the role of social intelligence in contemporary higher education. Moreover, it oﬀers practical applications that institutions can adopt to empower students with essential skills beyond academic knowledge, fostering well-rounded individuals equipped to address future demands. This study provides valuable insights for higher education institutions and educators. It underscores the need to prioritize the development of social intelligence within academic excellence, preparing students for the complexities of the modern world. Implementing strategies that enhance engagement, social presence, collaborative learning, and interactivity can signiﬁcantly foster social intelligence among students.

5.3 Limitations

This study’s limitation is the convenience sample size, where we only included students enrolled in a particular course within the industrial design program at the Monterrey campus. Future research can overcome this limitation by extending the study to

more courses within the industrial design program, including all semester courses that aim to develop social intelligence competency. This expansion would enable exploring the potential implementation of more collaborative activities in the industrial design program courses. By extending this research, we could gather more extensive data for analytical studies, leading to improved outcomes. Consequently, more reliable data interpretation would validate the hypotheses more eﬀectively.

6. Conclusion and Future Directions

In summary, the statistical analyses performed in this study validated the signiﬁcant impact of various factors (collaborative learning, engagement, social presence, and interactivity) on the development of social intelligence competency among higher education students. The ﬁndings support the hypotheses formulated in this research. Collaborative learning had the most signiﬁcant impact on developing social intelligence competency, followed closely by engagement and social presence. While interactivity exhibited a slightly lower impact, it still signiﬁcantly contributed to students’ cultivation of social intelligence. Psychological state signiﬁcantly inﬂuences our success and failure in life [79], the negative psychological eﬀects experienced by students during emergencies like COVID- 19 contributed to academic struggles, poor performance, and low self-conﬁdence. Developing social intelligence competencies, facilitated by collaborative learning, interactivity, social presence, and engagement, can be crucial in mitigating these negative eﬀects. Enhancing social intelligence through these means helps students adapt better to distance learning environments by fostering a sense of community, improving communication skills, and increasing motivation and engagement. Therefore, incorporating collaborative and interactive elements in distance education can support student’s psychological well-being and academic success during challenging times.

By substantiating these connections with statistical analyses and aligning the ﬁndings with established research, this study provides valuable insights for educators and institutions, emphasizing the need to prioritize these factors in educational strategies geared toward developing socially intelligent individuals. Future research can further investigate the relationship between the following factors and the development of social intelligence competency among higher education students:

 Longitudinal Studies: Conduct longitudinal research to assess the long-term effects of collaborative learning and social intelligence competency development. This can help determine whether the impacts observed are sustained over time and how they influence students’ professional and personal lives postgraduation.  Intervention Studies: Implement intervention-

based studies that introduce structured collaborative learning activities and measure their direct impact on social intelligence competency. This approach can yield practical insights and inform best practices for educators.  Psychological Factors: Investigate the psycholo-

gical mechanisms that underlie the relationship between collaborative learning and social intelligence development. Understanding how factors such as resilience, motivation, and anxiety influence these outcomes can provide a more nuanced perspective.  Technology Integration: Explore the role of

technology in facilitating collaborative learning and enhancing social presence and interactivity. Researching the effectiveness of various digital tools and platforms can provide insights into best

methodologies, such as interviews or focus groups, to capture students’ lived experiences. This can provide deeper insights into how students perceive and navigate collaborative learning environments and their influence on social intelligence.

By pursuing these directions, researchers can build upon the ﬁndings of this study and contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of the factors that enhance social intelligence competency among higher education students. This will ultimately support the development of eﬀective educational strategies that foster both academic success and psychological well-being.
