Mobile learning application characteristics and learners’ continuance intentions: The role of flow experience

Xue Yang1

Received: 19 December 2022 / Accepted: 22 May 2023 / Published online: 30 May 2023 © The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer Science+Business Media, LLC, part of Springer Nature 2023

Abstract Mobile devices have increased exponentially in the past decades and the rapid development of digital technology has provided rich soil for the growth of mobile learning (m-learning), which is a more convenient means of learning because of no limitations of space and time. Applying the stimulus-organismresponse (S–O-R) framework, this study explores how learners experienced flow during m-learning and how their flow experience led to their continuance intentions for m-learning. With an online survey of 270 valid respondents, the model used in this study was validated by the partial least squares method. The results show that two key characteristics of m-learning applications are positively related to flow experience, of which concentration, in particular, rather than perceived enjoyment, plays an important role in continuance intentions. The S–O–R framework offers an overarching way to explore the influences of m-learning application characteristics (stimuli) on learners’ evaluations of their m-learning processes (organisms), which further influence their continuance intentions (responses).

Keywords  Mobile learning · Flow · Perceived information fit-to-task · Perceived visual appeal · Continuance intentions

1  Introduction

Mobile devices have increased exponentially in the past decades and the rapid development of digital technology has provided rich soil for the growth of mobile learning (m-learning) (Al-Rahmi et al., 2022). M-learning allows students to learn through mobile devices providing greater convenience and flexibility (Mingyue

et al., 2022; Okai-Ugbaje et al., 2022). M-learning makes it possible to learn without face-to-face contact and avoid unnecessary social interactions. Learners can access the learning materials through the m-learning applications, unlimited by space and time (Diacopoulos & Crompton, 2020). Convenience and suitability make m-learning a new educational trend and increasing numbers of learners attempt to adopt m-learning (Pimmer et al., 2019).

The rapid growth of m-learning has drawn much attention from academic researchers, who have discussed some interesting topics (Granić, 2022; Kumar & Chand, 2019). A systematic review of studies on m-learning has shown a primary focus on the learner’s achievements (Crompton & Burke, 2018). The influences of m-learning on the learner’s performance and achievements have been widely explored (e.g., Chen & Hsu, 2020; Georgiou & Kyza, 2021). The results indicate that the lack of time and space limitations has made learning more convenient, thus supporting academic motivation and performance. The factors driving the acceptance of m-learning have also been discussed (Mutambara & Bayaga, 2021). For example, characteristics such as virtual reality applications can influence the learner’s preferences and even motivate further learning (Chen & Hsu, 2020). Hedonic and learning values were also found to be positively related to the learner’s use behaviors (Sitar-Taut & Mican, 2021).

Academic interest in the ability of m-learning to attract students to continue their learning has been increasing. However, the current understanding of the psychological processes that motivate continuance intention to learn is limited. Based on the stimulus-organism-response (S–O-R) framework (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974), this study aims to examine the psychological processes involved in flow experience during m-learning and how they contribute to the formation of continuance intentions. Specifically, the following research questions are proposed: RQ1. How do m-learning application characteristics influence learners’ flow experience in m-learning? RQ2. How does the learner’s flow experience subsequently influence the continuance intention? Stimulus refers to an external environmental cue that can draw an individual’s attention. Organism is related to the individual’s affective and cognitive internal conditions that intervene between the stimulus and their responses. Response refers to a behavioral outcome resulting from the organism. This study examined characteristics such as the information fit-to-task and visual appeal as the stimuli (S), the learner’s flow experience as the organism (O), and their continuance intentions as the response (R). This study argues that these two characteristics are critical for m-learning applications to evoke flow experience and continuance intentions.

There are two main theoretical implications. First, the S–O–R framework offers an overarching way to explore the influences of m-learning application characteristics on learners’ evaluations of their m-learning processes, which further influence their continuance intentions. Second, this study has extended flow theory to m-learning by showing that flow experience, of which concentration, in particular, rather than perceived enjoyment, plays an important role in cultivating continuance intentions.

2  Theoretical background

2.1  The S–O‑R framework

The S–O-R framework suggests that environmental stimuli could influence affective and cognitive processes, thus leading to certain responses. It has been argued that environmental cues could be manipulated to induce psychological processes that lead to certain behavioral responses (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974). This framework has been applied to studies on online consumer behaviors, such as purchase behaviors (Friedrich et al., 2019), consumer behavioral intentions (Bigne et al., 2020), and word-of-mouth intentions (Konuk, 2019).

Stimulus refers to an external environmental cue, which could be social, technical (Hu et  al., 2016), website content and design (Benlian, 2015), task-related, or aesthetic (Tang & Zhang, 2020). Such cues can draw an individual’s attention. Organism is related to the individual’s affective and cognitive internal conditions that intervene between the stimulus and their responses. The affective status reflects the feelings and emotions due to the stimulus. Cognitive status refers to the thoughts that arise during the information processing by which the individual converts the stimulus into useful information (Eroglu et al., 2001). Response refers to a behavioral outcome resulting from the organism.

The S–O–R framework was employed as the theoretical foundation of this study because it has already been widely used in prior research on online consumer behavior and has been recognized for its scientific merits in investigations of psychological processes and behavioral responses in virtual environments. M-learning application characteristics can be manipulated by the alteration of various informational and visual attributes, which act as stimuli to evoke affective and cognitive learning experiences, thereby influencing behavioral outcomes. The S–O–R framework offers an overarching way to explore the influences of m-learning application characteristics on learners’ evaluations of their m-learning processes, which further influence their continuance intention.

Thus, taking the S–O-R framework as theoretical foundation, this study examines the psychological processes involved in the flow experience during m-learning and contributing to the formation of continuance intentions. This study considered m-learning application characteristics as stimuli, the learner’s flow experience as organism, and the learner’s continuance intention as response.

2.2  M‑learning application characteristics as environmental stimuli (S)

The stimuli are related to the content and design features of an m-learning application. Technological features of virtual environments can affect a user’s experience positively or negatively (Animesh et al., 2011). Our study considered content features, such as information fit-to-task and design features that provide visual appeal as stimuli. Related to utilitarian value, information fit-to-task reflects the precision and appropriateness of the learning materials provided by the application to

facilitate efficient and effective learning. Visual appeal is related to hedonic value and refers to the learner’s perceptions of the application’s design aesthetics (Parboteeah et al., 2009).

2.3  Flow experience as organism (O)

Flow is a psychological state in which a learner is so immersed in an activity that they may forget about the real world around them (Csikszentmihalyi, 1975). Previous research has treated this state as a phase of an organism (An et  al., 2021; Tuncer, 2021). Flow experience has two critical dimensions: perceived enjoyment and concentration. In this study, perceived enjoyment refers to learners’ enjoyable and pleasurable states experienced during the learning process. Concentration reflects the extent to which the learner can be fully absorbed in the learning tasks.

M-learning has gained significant traction in the study of foreign languages, particularly in the study of English (Klimova, 2018). Flow in mobile game-based English learning can foster learner engagement (Chen & Hsu, 2020). As mentioned above, an environmental stimulus can evoke individuals affective and cognitive reactions (Eroglu et al., 2001). In m-learning context, perceived enjoyment and concentration reflect learners’ affective reactions and cognitive reactions, respectively. Thus, our study considered flow experience, including perceived enjoyment and concentration, as a phase of an organism because they are internal psychological processes responding to environmental stimuli.

2.4  Continuance intentions as response (R)

Learners’ continuance intentions are important behavioral outcomes regarding to m-learning. Previous research has suggested that learners’ continuance intentions could reflect actual behavioral outcomes (Mutambara & Bayaga, 2021). Following previous studies, this study treated a learner’s continuance intention as the response.

3  Hypotheses

3.1  M‑learning application characteristics (S) and flow experience(O)

Information fit-to-task and visual appeal are two critical characteristics of m-learning applications. Information fit-to-task reflects precision and appropriateness of learning materials provided by the m-learning application that can facilitate efficiently and effectively conducting learning tasks (Parboteeah et al., 2009). It reflects the quality of the learning resources provided by an m-learning application and is expected to be positively related to perceived enjoyment. Learners may face with the problem of information overload and must process much information to identify useful learning materials. If the learning resources provided by the m-learning application cannot fit the learners’ current learning tasks, then the search costs of

learners will increase when using the m-learning application (Guo et al., 2020; Lin et al., 2020). On the contrary, if the learning resource can fit learners’ current learning tasks, then they will have a pleasant learning journey. Furthermore, learners who perceive a high-level fit will have a better learning experience. When learners find that participation can achieve their learning objectives, they will find the learning process more enjoyable (Zhang et al., 2015). To investigate the relationship between the information fit-to-task characteristic of an m-learning application and the enjoyment perceived by the learner when using it, the following hypothesis was formulated:

H1: Perceived information fit-to-task is positively related to perceived enjoyment.

Perceived information fit-to-task is also believed to be positively related to concentration. If the learning materials presented by the m-learning application are irrelevant to the learning tasks, then the learners are likely to question their choices and be distracted from their studies. On the contrary, sufficient learning materials can stimulate learners’ interest in learning and make them focus on details of the learning content (An et  al., 2021). Moreover, learners may believe that using the m-learning application with the appropriate resources could help them achieve their learning targets and enhance their learning performance. Therefore, learners’ perceived information fit-to-task can dramatically enhance their perceived usefulness of the m-learning application (Davis, 1989), and perceived usefulness was positively related to flow experience (Ahmad & Abdulkarim, 2019). High levels of information fit-to-task reflect the high quality and expertise of the m-learning application (Dedeke, 2016), and high quality learning materials are more engaging than low-quality ones (Cuevas et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2016). To investigate the relationship between the information fit-to-task characteristic of an m-learning application and the level of concentration exhibited by the learner when using it, the following hypothesis was formulated:

H2: Perceived information fit-to-task is positively related to concentration.

Perceived visual appeal refers to learner’s perception of the design aesthetics of an m-learning application (Parboteeah et al., 2009). It is believed to be positively related to perceived enjoyment. A perceived high level of visual appeal can enhance the learner’s telepresence in the learning process, and learners who experience telepresence express an enjoyable and pleasant state (Pelet et al., 2017). Learners perceived high levels of visual appeal enjoyed themselves more in the learning process (Brannon Barhorst et al., 2021). What’s more, being involved in vivid visual situation can motivate affective attitude to the medium spontaneously (Zhang et al., 2015). So, being involved in a lively learning atmosphere can activate the pleasure of learning. To investigate the relationship between the perceived visual appeal of an m-learning application and the enjoyment perceived by the learner when using it, the following hypothesis was formulated:

H3: Perceived visual appeal is positively related to perceived enjoyment.

Perceived visual appeal is also expected to be positively related to concentration. Because the former increases the vividness of the learning materials provided by the m-learning application. Lively and colorful learning content can immerse the learner in current tasks and focus on what they are learning (Wang et al., 2020a). In addition, perceived high level of visual appeal can stimulate learners’ curiosity and enthusiasm for study (Chen & Hsu, 2020). Strong curiosity and enthusiasm motivate learners to concentrate on their learning (Dai et al., 2020). Moreover, a perceived high level of visual appeal can result in positive mood of learners during the learning process, and such positive mood can assist learners focus on their learning. To investigate the relationship between the perceived visual appeal of an m-learning application and the level of concentration exhibited by the learner when using it, the following hypothesis was formulated:

H4: Perceived visual appeal is positively related to concentration.

3.2  Flow experience (O) and continuance intention (R)

Flow experience has been found to result in specific behavioral outcomes (Ahmad & Abdulkarim, 2019; Mahfouz et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2020b). Perceived enjoyment and concentration are two dimensions of flow experience. Learners who enjoy their learning processes will become more fully involved in their learning processes. Perceived enjoyment in the learning process is a predictor of positive feelings in learners. Perceived high levels of enjoyment lead to learners’ good overall impressions and positive attitudes towards the m-learning application, and learners’ good overall impressions and attitudes are positively related to their continuance intentions (Rodríguez-Ardura & Meseguer-Artola, 2016). Furthermore, perceived high level of enjoyment can produce a high satisfaction level in learners (Hoffman & Novak, 1996), which, in turn, can also influence learners’ continuance intentions. Learners who are satisfied with the m-learning application would be willing to continue using them. Learners who enjoy the learning process are more willing to spend extra effort in study and more likely to use the m-learning application continuously. To investigate the relationship between the enjoyment perceived by a learner using an m-learning application and their continuance intention, the following hypothesis was formulated:

H5: Perceived enjoyment is positively related to a learner’ s continuance intention.

Concentration is also expected to be positively related to learners’ continuance intentions. When learners experience a concentration state in the learning process, they gain more autonomy and control over the pace of learning (Lee & Wu, 2017). Enjoying considerable autonomy in learning can lead to a strong desire to continue further study. Moreover, concentration on a learning task can improve

Furthermore, concentration is positively related to user satisfaction (An et al., 2021). If learners can focus on their studies, then they would tend to be more satisfied with their m-learning application and continue to use them. To investigate the relationship between the level of concentration exhibited by a learner when using an m-learning application and their continuance intention, the following hypothesis was formulated:

4  Methods

4.1  Research design

This study aims to examine the psychological processes involved in flow experience during m-learning and how they contribute to the formation of continuance intentions. It was primarily designed to be survey research. A survey study of a sample of the population can yield a quantitative description of the opinions of the population (Creswell, 2015). A cross-sectional survey was used in this study to gather opinion-related data from the actual users of m-learning applications. The partial least squares technique was utilized to validate the model in this study.

4.2  Data collection

An online survey website (www.​wjx.​cn) was employed to collect data. Marketing students in a Chinese university were invited to participate in a survey and were encouraged to share it with their friends and fellow students. The data collection period lasted from May 13 to 21, 2019. Finally, 325 responses were collected. Because 55 respondents reported no experience with m-learning apps, only 270

valid responses were acquired. The respondents with experience were also asked to name the applications they used. 37.8% of the respondents used the English learning applications, 35.9% of the respondents used the MOOC applications, and 26.3% of the respondents used other kinds of m-learning applications.

As shown in Table 1, 31.11% of the respondents were male and 68.89% were female. Most were younger than 30 and 87.78% had bachelor’s degrees. Most had been using m-learning applications for less than 2 years, 50.37% were using them several times per week, and most spent less than 1 h per time of use.

5.2  Structural model

As shown in Figure 2, perceived information fit-to-task is positively related to perceived enjoyment (β = 0.255, p < 0.01) and concentration (β = 0.228, p < 0.01). Thus, H1 and H2 are supported. Perceived visual appeal is positively related to perceived enjoyment (β = 0.394, p < 0.001) and concentration (β = 0.464, p < 0.001). Thus, H3 and H4 are supported. Contrary to expectations, perceived enjoyment has no effect on continuance intention (β = 0.136, p > 0.05). Thus, H5 is not supported. Concentration is positively related to continuance intentions (β = 0.498, p < 0.001). Thus, H6 was supported.

6  Discussion

Applying the S–O-R framework, this study examined the role of m-learning application characteristics and flow experience in cultivating learners’ continuance intentions in m-learning context. Specifically, this study explored the effects of m-learning application characteristics, that is perceived information fit-to-task and perceived visual appeal on flow experience, which in turn influence learners’ continuance intentions. The results of this study suggest that flow experience, especially concentration, plays an important role in the formation of intentions to continue m-learning.

First, the findings of this study present that m-learning application characteristics were positively related to flow experience. Specifically, perceived information fit-to-task had significantly positive effects on both perceived enjoyment and concentration. These results were consistent with previous studies. For example, Hsu et al. (2017) found that website quality was positively related to users’ flow experience. High information quality was an important aspect of website quality. Similarly, a high level of information fit-to-task was conducive to high quality in an m-learning application, thus enhancing the flow experience of learners. Additionally, perceived visual appeal was proven to be another important factor influencing perceived enjoyment and concentration. These findings are consistent with previous studies that have argued for perceived attractiveness being positively related to flow experience, especially focused attention (Liu, 2017). The visual attractiveness of an m-learning application can arouse a learner’ s attention when using the mobile application to study.

Moreover, contrary to expectations, this study found that perceived enjoyment did not affect learners’ continuance intentions for m-learning, which may indicate that learners employed m-learning applications for knowledge rather than for fun. Though interesting designs can improve learners’ perceived enjoyment during the learning process, perceived enjoyment cannot improve learners’ continuance intentions, thus possibly suggesting that learners used such applications for self-development. Hence, the purpose of m-learning is to acquire useful knowledge efficiently. If learners cannot concentrate on the learning materials, they will not continue to use them.

Finally, consistent with previous studies, concentration was found to have a positive effect on continuance intention. Previous studies have shown that flow experience is positively related to replay intentions of online games (Liu, 2017), media use (Pelet et al., 2017), satisfaction (Aboelmaged, 2018), purchase intentions (Hsu et al., 2017), and e-learning continuance (Rodríguez-Ardura & Meseguer-Artola, 2016). This study suggests that in the m-learning context, flow experience, especially concentration, is positively related to continuance intention. As mentioned above, if an m-learning application can provide sufficient and attractive resources, learners will continue to use it.

6.1  Theoretical implications

There are three theoretical implications. First, this study extends the existing research on m-learning by taking S–O-R framework as the theoretical foundation. The S–O–R framework offers an overarching way to explore the influence of

m-learning application characteristics (stimuli) on learners’ evaluations of their m-learning processes (organisms), which further influence their continuance intention to involve in the following m-learning (responses). The findings revealed that the m-learning application characteristics can be manipulated to evoke an immersive learning experience, which in turn influences their continuance intention. M-learning application characteristics had a positive influence on learners’ flow experience, and their further continuance intention.

Second, this study enhances the current understanding of flow theory by extending its applicability to the area of m-learning. Although flow theory has also been applied to explain human behaviors, such as games (Huang et al., 2018), addictions (Wang et al., 2020b), continuance intentions for SNS (Chang & Zhu, 2012), its ability to explain the processes involved in m-learning requires further investigation. This study has shown that flow theory could be employed as a theoretical lens for exploring the psychological processes of learners in m-learning context.

Third, this study has demonstrated the underlying psychological mechanisms involved in m-learning. The results have shown that flow experience, especially concentration rather than perceived enjoyment, plays an important role in cultivating learners’ continuance intentions. This study extends the current literature by indicating that concentration is an important missing link between m-learning application characteristics and learners’ continuance intentions.

6.2  Practical implications

This study has two important practical implications for m-learning application developers. First, the results have revealed that m-learning application characteristics are critical environmental cues that could evoke learners’ psychological processes in learning. Thus, design aesthetics should be emphasized by m-learning application developers when developing programs to attract a learner’s interest. Specifically, the learning materials provided by the m-learning application should meet the requirements of learning tasks. Rich and sufficient learning materials should be exhibited in a variety of forms, such as textbooks, knowledge points, mind maps, and quizzes. Presentations of the learning materials should be lively to keep a learner’s attention. A combination of texts, 2D and 3D pictures, videos, and live streaming were encouraged to exhibit the learning materials.

Second, perceived enjoyment was found to have no influence, whereas concentration was found to have positive effects on continuance intentions. This finding suggests that the purpose of m-learning is the efficient and effective acquisition of useful knowledge. Just having an enjoyable learning process cannot induce further learning interest. Thus, the design of an m-learning application should not contain irrelevant information. For example, too many animation sequences may be distracting, so an application’s design aesthetics should help learners focus on the core learning tasks.

6.3  Limitations and further research

This study has a few limitations. First, this study only focused on the m-learning behaviors of Chinese students. Although the results may be applied to nations

with comparable cultural origins, generalizations of the findings to other cultures should be cautious. Future studies can evaluate m-learning behaviors in different cultural settings. Second, most of the respondents in this study were undergraduates. Future studies can extend the current study to the context of elementary school and high school to find more interesting conclusion. Alternatively, future studies can also take continuing education as research setting and investigate factors that predict older adults’ m-learning intentions. Third, this study only considered informational and technical cues as environmental stimuli. Future studies can consider social cues because the social functions of m-learning applications may also be important driving factors of usage. Future studies can also consider the level of learning materials and the skill of the learners as control variables.

7  Conclusions

This study applied the stimulus-organism-response (S–O–R) framework to examine the roles of the characteristics of m-learning application and the flow experiences of learners in cultivating their continuance intentions for mobile learning. This study provides an overarching approach to exploring the effects of m-learning application characteristics (stimuli) on learners’ evaluations of their m-learning processes (organisms) and their continuance intention to involve in future m-learning (responses). The findings of this study have shown that m-learning application characteristics are positively related to flow experience. Contrary to expectations, this study found that perceived enjoyment had no effect on learners’ continuance intentions in m-learning. Moreover, this study found that concentration has a positive impact on learners’ continuance intentions.
