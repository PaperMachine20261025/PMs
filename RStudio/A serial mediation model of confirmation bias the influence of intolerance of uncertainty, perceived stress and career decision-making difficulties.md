## Abstract

**Purpose** – The purpose of this study is to find the susceptibility of confirmation bias and its relationships among intolerance of uncertainty (IU), perceived stress, and emotional-personality-related career decision-making difficulties (EPCD), through a serial mediation analysis.

**Design/methodology/approach** – The study employed a structural equation modelling (SEM) approach using R Studio (version 4.4.3). The study employed a cross-sectional design, involving 378 final-year undergraduate students from rural regions of three southern Indian states, who were selected through purposive sampling.

**Findings** – The findings revealed that students exhibit a tendency towards confirmation bias, and intolerance of uncertainty (IU) positively influences emotional and personality-related career decision-making difficulties (EPCD), as well as confirmation bias through perceived stress. Additionally, the results support the proposed serial mediation model, indicating that IU increases susceptibility to confirmation bias via its impact on perceived stress and, subsequently, EPCD.

**Research** **limitations/implications** – This study makes theoretical contributions to the fields of career decision- making and cognitive psychology, aligning with key Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly SDG 4 (quality education) and SDG 8 (decent work and economic growth). The findings offer actionable insights for researchers, career counsellors, and policymakers to design targeted interventions that reduce career decision- making difficulties and mitigate the tendency toward confirmation bias, thereby promoting more informed and quality decision-making.

**Originality/value** – This study extends existing models by introducing perceived stress and EPCD as serial mediators between intolerance of uncertainty and confirmation bias. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first study to explore this specific mediation pathway in the context of career decision-making among students from rural communities, providing valuable insights to the fields of cognitive psychology and career development.

**Keywords** Intolerance of uncertainty, Perceived stress, Career decision-making difficulties, Confirmation bias **Paper** **type** Research article

## 1. Introduction

Students and early-career professionals often face challenges in career choice due to fast- changing and unpredictable work environments, which pressure them to make the right decision in uncertain conditions ([Hirschi, 2012)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes). Many factors influence how individuals respond to uncertain situations, and one such key psychological factor is the intolerance of uncertainty (IU). This cognitive disposition (IU) is associated with information-processing biases that contribute to the development of excessive worry and anxiety ([Dugas](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [_et_](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [_al._](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[,](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [2005;](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [Carleton](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [_et_](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [_al._](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[,](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [2012)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its). It affects how individuals perceive, interpret, and respond to cognitive, emotional, and behavioural uncertainty. A previous study explored the link between self- reported IU and decisions in uncertainty-based behavioural tasks ([Carleton ](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[_et_](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[ ](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[_al._](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[, 2016)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its). Previous studies have found this tendency among various professionals, including software engineers ([Calikli and Bener, 2015)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its), forensic interviewers ([Melinder ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[ ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[, 2020)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). A study explored the susceptibility to confirmation bias in the health care context ([Piksa](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[,](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [2024)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). However, this study aimed to explore students’susceptibility to confirmation bias, as they may exhibit this behaviour by actively seeking out information that supports their preferred career choice, such as success stories and supportive opinions from parents and peers, while ignoring contradictory evidence, including job market saturation, constructive feedback, and misalignment with their skills. As a result, they may overlook critical factors needed for informed decision-making, potentially leading to career dissatisfaction or misfit. Over the past two decades, numerous studies have explored behavioural and cognitive biases in decision- making across various domains.

Research studies have found that postgraduate students pursuing professional courses are susceptible to bias blind spots and jumping-to-conclusions biases during the career exploration process. These studies highlight the relationship between cognitive biases and career exploration, though research on decision-making in this area remains in its early stages ([Priya ](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[_et_](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[ ](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[_al._](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[, 2025;](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [Priya and Vilvanathan, 2025)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes). Similarly, further studies examined the impact of behavioural biases on the career choices of management students ([Thapa](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [_et_](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [_al._](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[,](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [2025)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) and the susceptibility of confirmation bias among medical students during the diagnostic process ([Kojima ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[ ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[, 2025)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). In literature, confirmation bias and mood responses have been thoroughly examined in contexts such as politics and religion; has not been sufficiently explored, highlighting the need for further research in this area ([Dickinson,](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [2024)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its). There is a lack of research on IU within this context, accompanied by a noticeable shortage of targeted career interventions addressing this issue ([Lam and Santos, 2018)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato), [Gati ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_et_](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[ ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_al._](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[ (2012) ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)emphasised the need to investigate the relationship between EPCD and their behavioural consequences, highlighting how these factors influence the intensity and scope of counselling interventions required. Therefore, this study aims to explore the association between IU, perceived stress, EPCD, and confirmation bias using a serial mediation model with the SEM approach among final-year undergraduate students from the three southern states of India. ## 2. Literature review

### _2.1_ _Theoretical_ _background_

The Cognitive-Affective Conative model ([Kim](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [and](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [Chen,](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [2021)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) serves as the theoretical basis for this investigation. It describes how cognitive and emotional factors are activated sequentially, involving the psychological process of decision-making that influences an individual’s behaviour. When choosing a specialisation, selecting relevant courses, and exploring alternative career paths, students often encounter uncertain situations. Their intolerance for uncertainty frequently becomes overwhelming, leading to elevated stress and psychological discomfort ([Buhr and Dugas, 2002;](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [Carleton ](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[_et_](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[ ](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[_al._](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[, 2007,](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [2016)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its). It also intensifies anxiety, low self-efficacy, and career indecision ([Saka](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [and](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [Gati,](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [2007)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes). According to research, people are more likely to engage in motivated reasoning and biased information processing as coping mechanisms when they are faced with stress, anxiety, and uncertainty ([Proulx ](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[_et_](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[ ](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[_al._](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[, 2012)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes).

### _2.2_ _Hypothesis_ _development_

#### _2.2.1_ _Intolerance_ _of_ _uncertainty_ _(IU)_ _and_ _Confirmation_ _bias._ The rapid market changes have increased career instability, which is reflected in the growing diversity of career paths, specialised professions, and employment opportunities ([Schulz ](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[_et_](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[ ](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[_al._](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[, 2015;](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [Gati and Levin, 2014)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes). Intolerance of uncertainty (IU) refers to a trait of an individual ([Rosen](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [_et_](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [_al._](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[,](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [2014)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) and is a cognitive process that encodes and interprets information and responds to the world around them, characterised by a future-oriented element marked by unpredictability ([Grenier](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [_et_](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[ ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_al._](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[, 2005)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes). Prior research studies associate IU with impulsive, affect-driven decisions that favour immediate, suboptimal options, indicating impaired judgement under uncertainty, which may exacerbate emotional discomfort and cognitive strain ([Buhr and Dugas, 2002;](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [Dugas ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_et_](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[ ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_al._](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[, 2005;](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [Luhmann](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[,](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [2011)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). The studies show that IU significantly contributes to increased interpretation bias ([Tyson](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among) [_et_](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among) [_al._](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among)[,](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among) [2025)](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among), particularly in a social context ([Lauriola](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[,](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [2024)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). This study particularly focuses on confirmation bias, which is one of the behavioural biases that significantly contributes to the development of overconfidence, especially when people face uncertainty regarding a conclusion. It is the propensity to give more weight to data that

### K confirms one’s preexisting opinions while ignoring data to the contrary ([Rassin, 2008)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes). For 

instance, a previous study found that medical students are susceptible to confirmation bias during the rapid diagnostic process under time constraints ([Kojima ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[ ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[, 2025)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). Individuals are especially vulnerable to this bias under such conditions, leading them to be strongly inflexible in their initial beliefs even when presented with new, conflicting information, which can result in an overstated sense of confidence in their judgements and decisions ([Rabin](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [and Schrag, 1999)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes). With this argument, we propose:

_H1._ IU is positively associated with confirmation bias.

#### _2.2.2_ _Intolerance_ _of_ _uncertainty_ _(IU)_ _and_ _perceived_ _stress._ According to [Carleton ](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[_et_](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[ ](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[_al._](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [(2007,](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [2012),](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) IU represents discomfort with ambiguity, where uncertain situations are perceived as truly dangerous rather than just unclear. It frequently results in anxiety and an inability to act, which can be distressing and stressful. As a result, it considerably contributes to increased worry and trait anxiety ([Khawaja](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [and](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [Yu,](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [2010)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes). Those who struggle with high IU often feel anxious and uneasy ([Berenbaum](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [_et_](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [_al._](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[,](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [2008;](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [Chen](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [and](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [Zeng,](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [2021)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its), and it makes everyday situations feel overwhelmingly stressful ([Francis ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_et_](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[ ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_al._](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[, 2016)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes). A study shows the relationship between worry, IU and post-traumatic stress syndrome ([Bardeen ](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[_et_](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[ ](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[_al._](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[, 2013)](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes). Research consistently supports a strong association between IU and perceived stress ([Bardeen ](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[_et_](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[ ](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[_al._](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[, 2017)](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes), indicating that IU increases stress among people involved in a teacher training program ([Demirtas](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [and](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [Yildiz,](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [2019)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its). Perceived stress refers to an individual’s evaluation of a situation as stressful ([Coffman and Gilligan, 2002)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its). Stress responses arise only when a situation is seen as demanding and when the individual feels they lack the necessary resources to handle it ([Cohen and Hoberman, 1983)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its). Students frequently experience uncertainty when choosing a career path, which can lead to a great deal of stress and anxiety. These stress levels are further increased for some students by a perceived lack of readiness for their future careers ([Boo](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [and](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [Kim,](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [2020)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its). Studies also indicate that stress has a negative impact on students’ life satisfaction ([Coffman and Gilligan, 2002)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its). Thus, we assume [hypothesis 2.](#k-confirms-ones-preexisting-opinions-while-ignoring-data-to-the-contrary-rassin-2008-for) _H2._ IU is positively related to perceived stress.

#### _2.2.3_ _Intolerance_ _of_ _uncertainty_ _and_ _EPCD._ Many factors contribute to the complexity and increase the challenges that an individual experiences in the career decision-making process, such as uncertainty about oneself and the work environment, various career options and social barriers that often limit individuals’ career options ([Gati and Levin, 2014;](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [Krieshok ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[ ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[, 2009)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). Literature studies explained two types of difficulties in career decision-making: career indecision and career indecisiveness. Career indecision (career decision-making difficulties) involves cognitive factors such as lack of readiness, lack of information, and inconsistent information, whereas career indecisiveness (emotional personality related career decision- making difficulties- EPCD) involves affective factors such as emotional dysregulation, low confidence and decision-related anxiety ([Udayar ](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among)[_et_](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among)[ ](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among)[_al._](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among)[, 2020)](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among). This study specifically used EPCD because its affective factors align well with the theoretical framework of the Cognitive- Affective Conative model. According to a study, IU predicts decision-making difficulties and decisional safety behaviour ([Appel](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes) [_et_](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes) [_al._](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[,](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes) [2024)](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes), and it is positively associated with difficulties in making career decisions, including anxiety-induced inconsistencies in information, a lack of readiness, and inadequate information ([Arbona](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes) [_et_](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes) [_al._](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[,](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes) [2021)](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes). Another study explored the IU of students due to the post-COVID pandemic and its impact on the entrepreneurial mindset and career adaptability of higher education students ([Lee and Jung, 2021)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). From this discussion, we propose the following assumptions:

_H3._ IU is positively related to EPCD.

#### _2.2.4_ _Perceived_ _stress_ _and_ _EPCD._ People encounter career decision-making difficulties at various stages, such as before, during, or after making a career choice, as they are aware of the importance of their decision and worry about making an unsuitable one ([Anghel and Gati, 2021)](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes). It is a major concern among college students during their university studies ([Lipshits-](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)

[Braziler](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[,](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [2016)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). Research studies have shown that career decision-making difficulties are Kybernetes associated with depression, anxiety, stress, and worry ([Anghel and Gati, 2021)](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes). A study investigated how emotional intelligence and self-efficacy contribute to predicting difficulties in career decision-making ([Santos ](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[_et_](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[ ](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[_al._](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[, 2018)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes). Also, personality trait also reduces one’s ability to make career decisions ([Gati](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [_et_](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [_al._](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[,](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [2012)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes). It is highlighted that the increased stress can significantly disrupt an individual’s adaptive emotional regulation and cognitive processing ([Machulska](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [and](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [Klucken,](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [2025)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). [Rottinghaus](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [_et_](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [_al._](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [(2009)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [e](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)mphasised the need to investigate the potential influence of personal-emotional factors on aspects of career decision-making and found that students who had made a career decision were significantly less depressed. Thus, we propose the following hypothesis:

_H4._ Perceived stress has a positive effect on EPCD.

#### _2.2.5_ _Perceived_ _stress_ _and_ _confirmation_ _bias._ Perceived stress is an individual’s subjective experience of stress and the ability to manage it ([Qrmli,](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [2025)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes), and it is associated with biased information processing. This tendency will interpret environmental stimuli and ambiguous information in a negative manner ([Machulska and Klucken, 2025)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). Stressful life events are significantly associated with negative cognitive biases such as attentional, memory biases, dysfunctional attitudes and ruminative brooding ([Łosiak](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[,](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [2019;](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [Ford](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [and](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [Shook,](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [2019)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes). It frequently results in feelings of overwhelm and interferes with regular decision-making processes, causing people to prioritise quick, emotion-driven decisions ([Porcelli](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [and](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [Delgado, 2017)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes). Factors such as time pressure and information overload influence the stress environment of an individual. [Phillips-Wren ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[ ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[ (2019) ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)highlighted that individuals rely on cognitive shortcuts and exhibit heightened cognitive biases under stressful conditions. These conditions increase selective attention to and recall of belief-consistent information, which makes individuals susceptible to cognitive biases. With this, we propose: _H5._ Perceived stress is positively related to confirmation bias

These factors anticipate negative outcomes, emotional disturbances, and maladaptive cognitive perceptions that prevent students from making conclusive decisions ([Gati ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_et_](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[ ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_al._](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[, 2010;](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [Tian and Hou, 2023)](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among). ## 3. Methods

### _3.1_ _Participants_

The participants of this study comprise 55.5% females and 44.5% males. In terms of age, 89% of the respondents fall within the 20–22 age group, 9% are between 23–26 years, and 2% are above 26 years. The participants are final year students enrolled in undergraduate programs across various disciplines: 12% in arts, 30% in science, 37% in commerce, and 21% in management. In socio-economic research, annual family incomes are broadly defined and interpreted in relation to the locally established socio-economic status scales ([Masthi and Kulkarni,](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [2013)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). In this study, the participants belong to lower–to middle–income households located in rural areas of two Indian states. Rural communities are referred to as students enrolled in institutions located outside metropolitan and major urban regions. This population predominantly belongs to agriculture and related occupations ([Tilak and Choudhury, 2021)](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among). Regarding annual family income, 89% of the respondents come from families earning below ₹8 lakh (approximately US$9,400), while 11% come from families earning above ₹8 lakh. Additionally, 77% of the participants reported not having attended any career guidance program, whereas 23% indicated they had attended at least one program during their course.

### _3.2_ _Procedure_

In this study, we collected data using a mixed approach that combines both paper-and-pencil surveys and online questionnaires employing purposive sampling techniques. Students who belong to undergraduate courses in non-urban, rural areas were recruited for the study. We approached academic institutions located in rural regions and got permission to collect data from students. The paper surveys were administered in a classroom setting, where participants were first briefed on the purpose of the study. They were assured that their responses would remain confidential and were encouraged to ask questions or clarify any doubts before filling out the questionnaire. This process helped create a comfortable environment and encouraged better participation. Students who were not physically accessible were reached using an online survey through LinkedIn and emails. This increases the diversity of the sample and improves participation. Both modes used identical questionnaires to ensure measurement equivalence.

### _3.3_ _Ethical_ _considerations_

Before initiating the research, formal approval was obtained from the local institutional authorities. The approval process involved a thorough review of the research proposal to ensure compliance with ethical principles, including respect for participants, informed consent, confidentiality, and transparency. Participation was entirely voluntary, and students were selected accordingly.

### _3.4_ _Measure_ Kybernetes

_Intolerance_ _of_ _uncertainty_ _(IU):_ The Intolerance of Uncertainty Questionnaire ([Carleton](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [_et_](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [_al._](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its)[, 2007)](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) was used to assess IU. It comprises two sub-dimensions: prospective anxiety and inhibitory anxiety. This study specifically focuses on prospective anxiety (7 items), which evaluates an individual’s tolerance for potential future uncertainty. _Perceived_ _stress_: In the career decision-making context, it was measured using the 10-item scale of perceived stress, developed by [Cohen](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [_et_](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [_al._](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) [(1994).](#k-berenbaum-h-bredemeier-k-and-thompson-rj-2008-intolerance-of-uncertainty-exploring-its) This scale assesses the degree to which people perceive their lives as unpredictable and overloaded. The scale items were adapted to reflect stress specifically related to the career decision-making process. _Emotional_ _personality-related_ _career_ _decision-making_ _difficulties_ _(EPCD):_ The shortened, 11-item version of the EPCD Questionnaire ([Gati and Levin, 2014)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) was used to measure the sources of students’ general indecisiveness in career decision-making. The taxonomy of EPCD includes three dimensions: Pessimistic view, Anxiety, and self and identity ([Gati ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_et_](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[ ](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[_al._](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes)[, 2012)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes). _Confirmation_ _bias:_ Students’ susceptibility to confirmation bias in the career decision-making process was measured using a 10-item scale adapted from [Rassin](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [(2008).](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) The items assessed the tendency to seek, interpret, and recall information that confirms one’s existing beliefs or preferences regarding career choices, while discounting or avoiding disconfirming evidence. Sample item included for the reference: _“I_ _only_ _need_ _a_ _little_ _information_ _to_ _reach_ _a_ _good_ _decision_ _(related_ _to_ _career/job).”_ All construct items in the study were measured using a 7-point Likert-type scale, ranging from 1 5 Strongly Disagree to 7 5 Strongly Agree. Higher scores on each scale indicated a stronger presence of the respective construct. We did a pilot study with a sample of 134 participants to examine the psychometric adequacy and reliability of the study instruments rather than to test feasibility. [Hertzog](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) [(2008)](#dugas-mj-hedayati-m-karavidas-a-buhr-k-francis-k-and-phillips-na-2005-intolerance-kybernetes) notes that larger pilot samples are appropriate to obtain reliable and precise estimates of measurement properties.

### _3.5_ _Data_ _analysis_

### _3.6_ _Common_ _method_ _bias_

## For data collection, this study employed a self-report method, necessitating an assessment of the potential threat to validity posed by common method bias, also known as common method variance. The authors followed the procedures and statistical methods proposed by [Podsakoff ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[ ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[ (2012) ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)to eliminate this problem. Harman’s one-factor test was used to assess the common method bias. The results show that a single factor accounted for 37.9% of the total variance, which is below the recommended threshold of 50%, suggesting that common method bias is not a threat to this study. Additionally, we included the common latent factor (CLF) in the confirmatory factor analysis model, with all observed indicators loading on their respective theoretical constructs and the latent method factor. The introduction of the CLF indicated only slight changes in model fit compared to the baseline measurement model (CFI 5 0.954 versus 0.941; RMSEA 5 0.043 versus 0.047). Particularly, the main factor loadings remained largely consistent after the inclusion of the CLF. Overall, these results suggest that common method variance is not significantly biasing the study’s findings.

## 5. Discussion

This section discusses the theoretical, practical, and social implications of the study. The study makes a significant contribution to the literature on behavioural biases and career decision- making, as well as to the academic knowledge across domains where decision-making under uncertainty is critical. It also highlights the practical relevance of the findings for academicians, career counsellors, professionals and policymakers, and their potential to foster meaningful change at the societal level.

### _5.1_ _Theoretical_ _implications_

This study contributed to the cognitive–affective–conative model by showing that IU initiates a sequential process in which cognitive dispositions influence affective reactions, ultimately shaping behavioural biases. The research findings extend career development literature by

**Table** **4.** Mediation analysis

### K examining that uncertainty tolerance plays a key role in conditioning the dynamic interplay of 

cognitive dispositions, affective responses, and behavioural tendencies in career-related decision-making. This research empirically studied the connection between intolerance of uncertainty and confirmation bias in career-related decision-making processes, which reveals the direct and indirect effects of perceived stress, emotional and personality-related career decision-making difficulties (EPCD), that lead to susceptibility to confirmation bias. IU has a direct positive effect on confirmation bias, with indirect effects through perceived stress and EPCD, indicating partial mediation in two different paths. Furthermore, the research findings explored the significant serial mediation effect in the pathway IU → PS → EPCD → CB (β 5 0.108, SE 5 0.023, _p_ \< 0.001), which suggests that IU increases the stress among students, which in turn enhances career decision-making difficulties, leading to stronger confirmation bias. The cognitive-affective-conative model emphasises that decision-making is not purely rational but is influenced by cognitive and emotional processes, which leads to biased behaviours when people face ambiguous career decisions. This serial mediation highlights how cognitive and emotional factors interact and contribute to behavioural consequences in uncertain decision environments.

Exploring career information online is hardly under observation in this digitalised environment ([Amir ](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[_et_](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[ ](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[_al._](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes)[, 2008)](#table-a1-continued-kybernetes). This can be the cause of students’ susceptibility to biased information processing in career decisions and exploration. Specifically, this study focused on the role of prospective anxiety among students ([Wang](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among) [_et_](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among) [_al._](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among)[,](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among) [2022)](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among) and its impact on enhancing stress and career decision-making difficulties that lead to confirmation bias. The findings of the research align with [Rassin](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [(2008),](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) who clarifies that people are susceptible to confirmation bias under uncertainty. Lack of network, post-COVID impact on the economy, and past recessions may be the reasons for the tendency of intolerance to uncertainty and increased stress among students from rural or underdeveloped regions. In such conditions, they tend to rigidly stick to their initial beliefs, even when confronted with contradictory evidence, leading to an inflated sense of confidence in their decisions. These elements increase the difficulties of career choice and lead to biased information processing ([Trevor-Roberts,](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among) [2006)](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among). Also, people are becoming overconfident in their beliefs by misinterpreting information that contradicts them ([Rabin and Schrag, 1999)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes). [Kwok (2018) ](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)suggested that incorporating and normalising uncertainty within the career development process reflects a constructivist approach. This perspective encourages students to view uncertainty positively and develop strategies to manage it effectively.

[Rottinghaus](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [_et_](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [_al_](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)[.](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [(2009)](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes) [e](#porcelli-aj-and-delgado-mr-2017-stress-and-decision-making-effects-on-valuation-learning-kybernetes)mphasised that students who had made career decisions reported significantly lower levels of depression. This suggests that career clarity may play a protective role in enhancing emotional well-being. Furthermore, stress experienced during decision- making has been shown to shift cognitive processing from analytical reasoning to intuitive thinking, increasing reliance on habitual responses over deliberate, goal-directed actions. This cognitive shift heightens the risk of biased judgements ([Yu,](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among) [2016)](#k-tian-l-and-hou-z-2023-gender-discrimination-and-career-decision-making-difficulties-among). Although previous research has examined cognitive-emotional factors and behavioural biases separately in the context of career decision-making, the present study bridged the gap by investigating how these factors interact to influence susceptibility to confirmation bias. In doing so, the study aligns with SDG 4 - Quality Education by emphasising the need for educational interventions that enhance critical thinking and emotional resilience in career planning. Additionally, by addressing the psychological barriers and cognitive shortcuts to effective career decision-making, this research contributes to SDG 8- Decent Work and Economic Growth by supporting informed and mentally healthy transitions into the workforce, ultimately fostering a more productive and empowered labour force.

### _5.3_ _Social_ _implications_

A previous study highlighted that students from low-income, underserved communities who are all studying in rural schools lack access to career-related information, training options, and adequate hands-on learning ([Lindstrom](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_et_](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [_al._](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato)[,](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato) [2022)](#k-kojima-j-uehara-t-ohira-y-tsukamoto-t-shikino-k-yokokawa-d-li-y-yanagita-y-sato). It explains that the family’s social class has a significant influence on young people’s career development. In this study, the authors highlight how psychological challenges faced by students from rural or countryside regions can increase their susceptibility to confirmation bias. For example, such students often rely on culturally reinforced career beliefs, such as viewing government jobs as the only secure option or limiting career choices based on traditional gender roles, rather than objectively exploring a broader range of opportunities. These insights underscore the urgent need to improve access to structured career counselling and mental health support within rural educational institutions in developing countries. Promoting a stronger sense of self-concept and self-awareness among students can help reduce their vulnerability to cognitive biases and enhance their ability to make informed career choices. The study’s findings offer valuable direction for educators, policymakers, and community stakeholders seeking to foster more resilient, reflective, and empowered decision-makers in under-resourced settings.

## 6. Limitations and future research

Although the study contributes theoretical and practical implications, it has its limitations as well, which can guide future research. The study explored the susceptibility of confirmation bias among students during their career decision-making process, particularly when they face challenges in making decisions. It provided suggestions to implement career interventions, bias mitigation strategies, and mindfulness training programs, but it did not provide practical methods to suggest. The participants for the study were chosen from rural parts of three Indian states, which is not a representation of the entire population. Also, this is a cross-sectional, self- reported questionnaire that was used for data collection, which limits the ability to infer causality. More experimental and longitudinal studies are needed to develop mitigation or intervention strategies to improve the students’ decision-making quality in career-related

## K concerns, which could reveal whether confirmation bias diminishes with increased exposure 

to decision-making scenarios. For instance, structured reflection tasks, premortem analysis, or training in metacognitive strategies may help reduce biased reasoning. Testing such interventions in real-world educational and counselling settings can offer practical implications for improving student decision quality. Future studies should investigate how these cognitive and emotional factors vary across different socio-cultural and geographic settings. For example, rural versus urban backgrounds and collectivist versus individualist cultures may help to better understand how cultural context amplifies or mitigates decision difficulties and confirmation bias. Along with that, other behavioural and cognitive biases can be explored, and how these biases interact with career decision-making difficulties. Upcoming studies can explore how other SDG goals, such as poverty and inequalities, interact with these elements. With students increasingly relying on digital platforms and algorithm-driven content, future research should explore how such environments reinforce biased career beliefs. Finally, more potential variables can be included, such as peer support and social support, which can be treated as a moderating variable in future studies. The findings are especially valuable for career counsellors, educators, and policymakers, as they point to specific psychological barriers that hinder optimal decision- making. By addressing these barriers, interventions can be developed to foster more reflective and rational decision-making processes, thus improving career outcomes and individual well-being.
